Power Analysis Check

No power analyses were detected.

Power analyses need to contain the following information to be interpretable: the type of power analysis, the statistical test, the software used, sample size, critical alpha criterion, power level, effect size, and an effect size metric. In addition, it is recommended to make sure the power analysis is reproducible (by sharing the code, or a screenshot, of the power analysis), and to provide good arguments for why the study was designed to detect an effect of this size.

For an a-priori power analysis, where the sample size is determined, reporting all information would look like:

An a priori power analysis for an independent samples t-test, conducted using the pwr.t.test function from pwr (Champely, 2020), indicated that for a Cohen’s d = 0.5, an alpha level of 0.05, and a desired power level of 80% required at least 64 participants in each group.

For a sensitivity power analysis, this sentence would look like:

A sensitivity power analysis for an independent samples t-test, conducted using the pwr.t.test function from pwr (Champely, 2020), indicated that with 64 participants in each group, and an alpha level of 0.05, a desired power level of 80% was reached for an effect size of d = 0.5.

How Ethics Approval Check works

This module searches for statements of ethics approval, IRB approval, institutional review board approval, and related terms.

Patterns are designed to capture:

Human research committee names - Institutional Review Board / IRB (US standard) - Independent Ethics Committee / IEC (ICH-GCP international clinical trial term) - Ethical/Ethics Review Board (generic) - Ethics (Advisory/Review) Committee/Board/Panel/Sub-committee - Research Ethics Committee/Board/Panel (UK/Canada/Australia) - Medical Ethics Committee - Human Subjects/Research Committee / Human Research Ethics (Advisory) Panel - Committee for/on the Protection of Human Subjects - Committee on (Health) Research Ethics - Office of Research Ethics - Abbreviations: IEC, REC, REB, ERB, METC, DEC. REC/REB/ERB/DEC are short enough to collide with unrelated jargon (e.g. REC as a recognition-heuristic model abbreviation, DEC as a decision-task label, Reb as an author surname), so these four require an ethics/approval/committee/board/panel/institutional/ protocol word within ~30 characters in the same sentence. IRB and IEC are kept unconstrained as they are not observed to collide in practice. - Dutch: Medisch-ethische toetsingscommissie (METC) - French: Comité d'éthique / Comité d'Ethique

Animal research committee names - Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee / IACUC (US standard) - Any sentence containing animal(s) and committee (or board/panel) within ~5 words of each other — catches Experimental Animal Care and Use Committee, Animal Ethics Committee, Animal Welfare Board, Animal Welfare Ethical Review Body, etc. - UK: Animal Welfare Ethical Review Body / AWERB - Dutch: Dierexperimentencommissie / DEC (see DEC constraint above); Instantie voor Dierenwelzijn / IvD - German: Tierversuchskommission / TvK (advisory committee under §15 Tierschutzgesetz) - French: Comité d'éthique en expérimentation animale / CEEA; Comité d'éthique animale - Spanish: Comité de ética en experimentación animal / CEEA

Approval phrasing - Ethics/Ethical (approval|clearance) [was] (granted|obtained|received|given|secured) - Ethically approved - Approved by the (ethics|irb|institutional|independent|local|review|research) ... - This/The study/protocol/experiment/procedures/methods/research [was] (approved|reviewed) by - Study was ethically approved - (Followed|met|follow) the ethical (guidelines|standards|requirements) - In accordance with ... ethical (guidelines|standards) - Ethics protocol - Approved under (protocol|reference|number)

Waiver / exemption - Ethics/Ethical (waiver|exemption) - IRB (waiver|exemption|exempt) - Exempted from (ethics|irb|institutional review) - Waived by the (ethics|irb|institutional|review) (committee|board) - Deemed exempt

Declaration of Helsinki - Declaration of Helsinki / Helsinki Declaration

Patterns are NOT designed to capture: - General ethical considerations or ethical dilemmas in study content - Mentions of “ethics” in theoretical or philosophical contexts - Author approval of a manuscript (e.g. “all authors approved the final version”) - Ethical behaviour as a measured variable (e.g. “ethical decision-making”) - URLs containing “ethics” in path components

Live data collection is detected by the internal helper .detect_live_data(), which searches for sentences indicating that data were collected directly from human or animal participants. Papers without any live-data signal are not flagged as missing ethics approval. The helper uses conservative patterns to avoid flagging secondary data analyses, computational experiments, or economics papers that analyse existing survey data. See .detect_live_data() for the full list of patterns and intentional exclusions.

A paper that states that no human or animal subjects took part (“no human subjects were involved”, “did not involve human participants”, or people taking part “not as subjects”) is not expected to have an ethics statement, whatever participant phrase it also has (see .detect_no_participants()). A negation near a participant phrase alone (“No participants were excluded”) does not count: it reports a study with participants.

How Power Analysis Check works

This module uses regular expressions to identify sentences that contain a statistical power analysis. If specified by the user, it also uses a large language model (LLM) to extract information reported in power analyses, including the statistical test, sample size, alpha level, desired level of power, and magnitude and type of effect size.

The Power Analysis Check module uses regular expressions to identify sentences that contain a statistical power analysis. Without the use of an LLM, the module uses regular expressions to classify the power analysis as a-priori, sensitivity or post-hoc. With the use of an LLM, it checks if the power analysis is reported with all required information.

The regular expressions can miss power analyses, or fail to classify them correctly. The type of power analysis is often difficult to classify, which can easily be solved by explicitly specifying the type of power analysis as ‘a-priori’, ‘sensitivity’, or ‘post-hoc’. Note that ‘post-hoc’ or ‘observed’ power is rarely useful. The LLM can fail to identify information in the paper, and will not have access to information in paragraphs in the paper other than those that contain the word ‘power’. This package was validated by the Metacheck team on articles in Psychological Science.

How StatCheck works

Check consistency of p-values and test statistics

The Statcheck module runs Statcheck. Statcheck searches for regular expressions that match a predefined pattern, and identifies APA reported statistical tests. More information on the package can be found at https://github.com/cran/statcheck. The module only returns Statcheck results for t-tests and F-tests, as these are the only tests which have been validated, see https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/tcxaj_v1/.

Statcheck was developed by Michèle Nuijten and Sascha Epskamp.

Statcheck considers p = 0.000 an error, as you should report p < 0.001. Furthermore, p < 0.03 is an error if the p-value was 0.031, and one should simply report exact p-values (p = 0.031). Statcheck might miss one-sided tests, and falsely assume the p-value is incorrect. For more information, see StatCheck.

This module only checks statistical results reported in the running text of the manuscript. It cannot (yet) process statistics reported only in tables.

How Exact P-Values works

List any p-values reported with insufficient precision (e.g., p < .05 or p = n.s.) or reported as exactly zero (e.g., p = .000).

This module uses regular expressions to identify p-values. It will flag any values reported as p > ? or p < numbers greater than .001. It will also flag p-values reported as exactly zero (e.g., p = .000, p = 0.00), which are mathematically impossible — p-values are never exactly zero and should instead be reported as p < .001.

A p-value that is not the result of a test is not flagged: a table note (“* p < .05, ** p < .01”), a figure legend (“Asterisks indicate significant differences (p < .05)”, “Values in bold are significant at p < .05”) or a stated threshold (“p < .05 was considered significant”, “thresholded at p < .001”). GROBID often puts such notes into the running text, so each p-value is judged by the words in its own clause (the text up to a “;” or a full stop), and never left out when a test statistic belongs to it (“F(1, 7) = 8.03, # p < .05”). The column not_a_test of the table says why a p-value was left out (“marker”, “threshold” or “legend”). Notes in other forms, such as a marker after the p-value (“p < .05 (*)“), are still flagged.

This module sometimes flags false positives when sentences discuss how p-value thresholds were set. It can also miss an imprecise p-value that is reported against a threshold (“decreased at the p < .05 significance threshold”).

This module only checks p-values reported in the running text of the manuscript. It cannot (yet) process p-values reported only in tables (as opposed to a table’s footnote text, which the module does see and tries to exclude — see above).

This module was developed by Lisa DeBruine and Daniel Lakens

Read more in the metacheck manual.

Validation: In a sample of 225 papers containing 405 instances of non-exact p-values, the module correctly detected 269 cases (true positives) and incorrectly identified 78 (false positives). It missed 136 instances of imprecisely reported p-values (false negatives) and correctly identified 4557 cases of precisely reported p-values (true negative). Additionally, 78% of positive detections were correct (positive predictive value).

How Codebook Check works

This module checks whether the data columns in a repository are documented in a codebook or README. It locates codebook/readme files, extracts the variable definitions they contain, matches those definitions against the data columns extracted by data_check, and reports documentation coverage: how many data columns are documented, and which documented variables are never used.

The Codebook Check module consumes the columns and file classification produced by data_check (and, transitively, repo_check). Files classified as codebook or readme are parsed with parse_codebook(), which reads structured tables (CSV/TSV/Excel with a variable-name column and a label column), embedded labels in haven files (SPSS/Stata/SAS), and plain text from rich-text formats (docx/pdf/rtf/odt). Embedded haven labels from data files are also harvested directly.

Each data column is then matched against the parsed variable definitions with match_column_labels(), using normalised-name matching with experiment-group scoping, haven-label priority, and rule-based label-equivalence merging.

Beyond the variable label, the module harvests the DDI-style per-variable properties a source supplies: the value labels / code list (the 1=“Strongly disagree”…5=“Strongly agree” mapping — from SPSS/Stata value labels and from a codebook “values”/“coding” column), the missing-value scheme (which codes denote missingness, from haven declared missings and labels that read as “refused”/“n/a”), and question text and columns when present. These are carried onto the matched data columns and exported into the Psych-DS variableMeasured (as a schema.org code list plus namespaced metacheck: fields).

The module defaults to rules-only when llm_use(FALSE). When llm_use(TRUE), three optional LLM tiers run: parsing unstructured codebook text that the rules could not handle, fuzzy-matching still-unlabelled columns to still-unmatched codebook variables, and merging conflicting label definitions into a single canonical label.

How Repository Check works

This module retrieves information from repositories.

The Repository Check module lists files on the OSF, GitHub, ResearchBox, DSpace (PsychArchives and other legacy- and DSpace 7-based installations), Zenodo, Dataverse, Figshare, Dryad, ReShare, 4TU.ResearchData, Mendeley Data, DataONE (Arctic Data Center, KNB, and other member nodes), and LDbase based on links in the manuscript.

Listing a repository’s files never requires an API key, but some hosts answer faster or more completely with one: OSF allows 100 requests/hour unauthenticated versus 10,000/day with a token (see [osf_pat()]), and Dryad enforces a strict per-IP daily quota on repeated listing (see cache below). Dryad is a special case beyond rate limits: listing a Dryad dataset’s files works without a key, but actually downloading file CONTENTS from Dryad fails with HTTP 401 unless a key is set, even for a fully public dataset (see [dryad_pat()] or [dryad_auth()]). Since this module only lists files and never downloads their contents itself, that limitation applies to data_check and code_check (which do download content), not here – see their own documentation.

When a linked OSF page is a registration, its registered_from project (the one it was registered from, which the manuscript itself may never link directly) is also checked: if that project is public its files are listed under its own URL, and if it is closed or otherwise inaccessible the report flags it explicitly, since some registrations only describe their content rather than mirroring it.

How Code Check works

This module retrieves information from repositories checked by repo_check about code files (R, Python, SAS, SPSS, Stata, Mplus, MATLAB).

The Code Check module checks R (also R Markdown and Quarto), Python (also Jupyter notebooks), SAS, SPSS, Stata, Mplus, and MATLAB files, using regular expressions to check the code; JASP files are listed but not read. The language of a Quarto document or a notebook is read from the file itself (its engine or kernel), not from its extension. The regular expression search will detect the number of comments, the lines at which libraries/imports are loaded, attempts to detect absolute paths to files, setwd() and install.packages() calls, and lists files that are loaded, and checks if these files are in the repository; R code is also parsed. The module will return suggestions to improve the code if there are no comments, if libraries/imports are loaded in lines further than 4 lines apart, if files that are loaded are not in the repository, if absolute file paths, setwd() or install.packages() are found, and if R code cannot be parsed.

The regular expressions can miss information in code files, or falsely detect parts of the code as a fixed file path. Libraries/imports might be loaded in one block, even if there are more than 3 intermittent lines. The module was tried out internally on papers published in Psychological Science, but has not been formally validated. There might be valid reasons why some loaded files can’t be shared, but the module can’t evaluate these reasons, and always gives a warning.

The module also checks whether the repository pins the R/package versions the analysis actually depended on: an renv.lock file (parsed for the R version and every locked package + its version/source), a sessionInfo()/sessioninfo::session_info() text dump (matched by filename — sessionInfo.txt/session_info.txt and similar, or embedded in a README), or a groundhog::groundhog.library()/checkpoint::checkpoint() date-pin call in the code (a bare library(groundhog)/library(checkpoint) does not count — the pinning call itself must be present). When none of these is found, the report flags it as a reproducibility gap: package versions may drift between when the analysis was run and any later reproduction attempt.

If you want to extend the package to perform additional checks on code files, or make the checks work on other types of code files, reach out to the Metacheck development team.

Checking code requires downloading its file contents. For a paper whose repository is on Dryad, this never succeeds unless a Dryad API key is set: Dryad requires one to download file bytes, even from a fully public dataset (listing the repository’s files does not need one). Without a key, every Dryad-hosted code file is listed but unchecked, and a warning naming Dryad and ?dryad_pat/?dryad_auth is included among the download failures recorded in the manifest.

How Data Check works

This module classifies the files in a repository into semantic types (data, codebook, code, software, output, supplemental, readme, asset, other) and, for tabular data files, extracts each column’s type and summary statistics.

The Data Check module consumes the file list produced by repo_check (or any files added via local_path). Each file is classified with rule-based heuristics layered over metacheck’s file_category() and file_types: file names matching README / codebook patterns win first, then an extension crosswalk, then format-locked extension overrides. For every file classified as tabular data that is available locally, the module reads the file head, describes each column with data_col_facets() and computes summary statistics for numeric columns. Every sheet of a spreadsheet that holds a table is read: a file with several such sheets has its columns under source_file = "file.xlsx [sheet]", and the sheet column names the sheet of any spreadsheet with several sheets; a sheet of notes or a codebook is treated as documentation when the file has another table. Following DDI, a column is described by orthogonal facets rather than a single type: representation (numeric/text/datetime/code — how it is stored), measurement_level (Stevens: nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio), concept (what it measures: reaction_time/age/gender/likert/id/…), role (identifier/measure/condition/timestamp), unit, and a quality state (ok/empty/constant). Concepts are detected by name+value rules (the demographic concepts via data_check_demographic()); when llm_use(TRUE) the model fills concepts and measurement levels the rules left blank. Qualtrics survey exports are recognised (data_check_is_qualtrics()): their extra header rows are stripped so columns type correctly, and the reserved response-metadata columns (StartDate, Duration, Finished, …) are tagged.

The module defaults to rules-only when llm_use(FALSE): columns the rules cannot resolve (ambiguous 3–20-unique integers, ambiguous character columns) fall back to continuous / text respectively. When llm_use(TRUE), ambiguous columns and unresolved file types (other) are sent to llm() for optional refinement, and every file is assigned a study group (ex1, pilot2, shared, …) so that multi-study repositories can be recognised (used by psychds_check). Without an LLM the study group is left NA (unknown).

Column extraction requires files to be readable from disk. Files fetched by repo_check from OSF/GitHub/etc. without a local copy (file_location is NA) are classified but not column-extracted; pass local_path to point at a downloaded copy of the repository.

For a paper whose repository is on Dryad, this means column extraction never happens unless a Dryad API key is set: Dryad requires one to download file bytes, even from a fully public dataset (dataset listing and metadata do not need one). Without a key, every Dryad file stays classified but un-downloaded, column_n is 0, and a warning naming Dryad and ?dryad_pat/?dryad_auth is included among the download failures. A one-time session message is also shown (see repo_check) the first time a Dryad dataset is encountered with no key set.

Every downloaded text file the authors wrote (code, settings, survey definitions such as Qualtrics .qsf files, documentation, outputs) is searched for keys: API keys, access tokens and private keys, which let anyone who downloads the file use a paid service at the authors’ cost. Of a tabular data file only the column names and first 3 rows of the table as read are searched: its values come from participants, but a key the authors set for a whole survey (as embedded data in a Qualtrics survey flow) is exported in every response. The search uses the rules of the secret scanner betterleaks (https://github.com/betterleaks/betterleaks, MIT licence) of high and medium confidence. A key found turns the traffic light red; the report and the keys table give its file, line and type, and only its beginning. Other files over 25 MB and binary files are not searched. A key is never tested with its provider. Every module’s result has any whole key replaced by “keyremoved” (see module_run()).

How Reproducibility Check works

This module assesses whether a paper’s R and Python code can be run on its data. It works in two phases. The static phase (always run) collects the package dependencies the code declares, rewrites the data-file paths that do not work in the repository as the authors organised it, works out the order the scripts must run in, and diagnoses why any referenced input file is unavailable — reporting what a reproduction attempt would involve and where it is likely to break. The execution phase (opt-in, execute = TRUE) then actually runs the code: it copies the repository into a throwaway directory, optionally installs the declared dependencies into a throwaway library, runs each script in order in an isolated subprocess with a per-script timeout, and captures the outcome and full output of each run.

By default (execute = FALSE) the module does not run any code — it performs only static analysis: reading dependencies with the same extractors as code_check, finding the files the code references in the repository, and building a run-order dependency graph. It reports what would be installed and run rather than doing it.

When execute = TRUE, the module additionally runs the downloaded code on your machine — a deliberate, opt-in action gated behind that argument (callr is required for R code). Each script runs in an isolated subprocess so a crash cannot take down the R session; note this isolates crashes, not the filesystem or network. Installs (when install_missing = TRUE) and the throwaway run library never touch your main R library. Each script’s outcome is one of ran_ok, errored, timed_out, dependency_unavailable, not_parsed, run_by_source (an R script another script runs with source(), which is run only inside that script, since on its own it lacks the packages and objects the sourcing script sets up first), run_by_import (a Python file other files import, a module rather than a script, unless it has an if __name__ == "__main__": block), jags_model (a JAGS or BUGS model saved with an R extension, which another script reads) or not_r_content (a file that is not R at all, such as a JSON or HTML dump); these last two are not run and are not failures. python2 marks Python 2 code that was not run. With sandbox = "docker" such code runs with Python 2.7 (option metacheck.docker_image_py2; FALSE turns this off), which modifications records; it is never converted, since that can change results. defines_functions is a file that only defines functions, which is used, not run (table$used_by names the scripts that use it), and not_analysis is a file of software rather than of the analysis: a package’s code, tests, documentation or setup.py, a copy of a tool, a web application, a script a program wrote (table$not_analysis_why says which); neither is run or counted as a script. docker_failed means the container never started, which says nothing about the script, so it keeps the light from being green but does not make it red. needs_a_person is a script that stopped because it asks a person to choose a file or answer a question, or that starts an app, and needs_internet one that stopped because the network is off in a Docker run, or, with internet = TRUE, could not reach what it asked for; download_too_large is a script stopped for downloading more than the limits allow (see internet), and out_of_memory one that, or a program it started (such as a compiler), ran out of the memory each script may use. All four are treated the same way. not_downloaded is a script that stopped on a file the repository lists but that was not downloaded, such as one over max_file_size: it could not be tried, so it is not counted as a script that did not reproduce (summary_table$repro_not_downloaded_n counts them apart). not_read is a file metacheck could not read, so it was not run; the light is then not green, as the file may read and run on another computer, and table$error_message says why, naming a package that is installed but cannot be loaded on the computer that ran the check. empty is a file with nothing in it, which is not run or counted as a script. For a script that did not run through, table$error_message is its error, short (at most 300 characters): the error line of its output, which names a package that is not there, with the address it asked for when it needed the internet, or the time limit it reached; table$reason sorts it into a kind. The full stdout/stderr of every run is kept in the result’s run_results (the report shows it in per-script dropdowns, capped for readability). The traffic light reflects the STATIC readiness signals by default, but an execution error (errored/timed_out) does recolour it to red — a script that crashes on its own analysis is a genuine reproduction failure. A script that fails with could not find function is run once more with the library() calls the project’s other scripts make added at its top (or the one package that exports the function, or magrittr for %>%), and a script that then runs is reported as reproducible only after that repair.

Dependencies are reported name-only, with the source (CRAN, GitHub, or a base package already shipped with R) — static analysis of the code cannot know which version was used, and running against current package versions is itself informative: a break argues the authors should have pinned versions. Abstract

Psychology, economics, and behavioral science catalog human judgment through hundreds of disparate cognitive biases. The Arena Model (AM) replaces this disjointed taxonomy with a single account: judgment runs on a low-dimensional projection of a higher-dimensional world. The model derives many canonical heuristics and biases from a few fixed mechanisms. A "bias," per the AM account, is what happens when a mind works with fewer dimensions than its environment contains, when a researcher measures dimensions that differ from what the mind used, or when an internal signal is taken to report a property of the world. Many apparent departures from rationality resolve once unmeasured and mismatched dimensions are taken into account. Unlike descriptive frameworks that add a new mechanism for each phenomenon, the AM constrains the space of possible errors and yields clear, falsifiable predictions about the conditions under which biases will and will not appear. It also gives researchers a single, testable account in place of a growing list of unconnected exceptions. I demonstrate consistency across known biases, define the limits of the account, and propose a diagnostic test to evaluate, or invalidate, it.

1 Introduction

Expansive volumes of research catalog how human judgment deviates from normative models [72, 27]. Unifying accounts of judgment are much rarer. The Arena Model (AM) addresses this gap by treating judgment as systematic motion through a high-dimensional space (the arena), with biases arising from misinterpretations or violations of that space. Fields fill the arena, but they only describe the space. What acts on the fields to form judgments, and what gets left out when they are read, are what generate the heuristics and biases documented by the judgment and decision making (JDM) catalog.

The meat and drink of the AM is that catalog. It is a monumental empirical achievement that impresses equally with its hundreds of named, many of which are replicated, departures from normative standards and the cleverness of empirical manipulations that isolate them. A result of remarkable enthusiasm for JDM science, it is now a catalog of overwhelming size that forfeits the very clarity its entries are meant to provide. Each entry functions as a primitive explanandum, separate from any shared, more basic derivation.

Relatedly, JDM models are frequently simplified to a degree that undermines their descriptive abilities. Real sources of variance are absorbed into "noise," not directly modeled [43]. The AM account is, among other things, a proposal about where some of that variance lives. It is not an editorial correction to the catalog, nor is it an argument for how to accomplish that process. It is a reframing of how we think about judgment that forces a reconciling of the JDM catalog.

1.1 Foundational prior art

Situating the AM account against what its neighbors already supply will make its committed divergences more intelligible. Lewin's psychological field theory [45] claims that human behavior is the result of interacting psychological forces within and across a person's total psychological environment. A fundamental difference between the two accounts is that fields alone do not constitute the AM. The model only becomes a meaningful theory under the weight of its defined operations, functionals, and equipment. They are what afford the AM its ability to recover catalog entries and predict the conditions under which new anomalies should appear.

The heuristics-and-biases tradition supplies the catalog and much of the observational vocabulary this paper works from. The named entries, their manipulations, and the standards against which they are scored can all be traced to this tradition [72]. The AM account differs by declining to take those entries as primitive. It treats them as explananda for derivation, not as the theoretical bedrock. The adaptive-toolbox and ecological-rationality traditions supply the insight that apparently poor judgment is often well-adapted to the statistics of a real environment, and that a standard can itself be mismatched to the task [27]. The AM concedes that insight for one class of phenomena ( §2.5), but does not extend it to the whole catalog.

Decision Field Theory supplies a dynamic, formal account of deliberation. It is an explicit process model of how preference accumulates over the course of a single choice [10]. The AM operates at a more fundamental level: it models the substrate that generates the ensemble of episodes deliberation draws from. The AM also differs from quantum cognition, which supplies a formalism flexible enough to fit a wide range of observed anomalies, including order effects and violations of classical probability [9]. Where the quantum cognition approach is to accommodate anomalies through formal flexibility, the AM seeks to derive them from a compact parameter set.

Free-energy and active-inference accounts supply a top-down governing principle for cognition and action: the minimization of surprise or prediction error [23]. The AM takes the opposite formal direction and serves a different purpose: it starts from primitives and builds upward, yielding a generative capability that produces testable predictions ( §3.5).

1.2 The commitments

An architecture that runs upward is the core structural commitment of the AM. Model primitives generate composites, and composites (in some cases, the primitives directly) generate the JDM catalog phenomena. Everything that follows executes this claim. Section 3 establishes the AM functionally, from its primitives through to the composites assembled from them. Section 4 demonstrates how catalog phenomena are derived from, not posited by, the AM. Section 5 shows what the AM account forbids. Most substantively, transformations along dimensions not recruited for a judgment are predicted to leave it unchanged. Accepting the primitives implies a commitment to what follows from them. Voicing a doubt about the AM explaining a downstream phenomenon requires pointing to which primitive or composite the doubt strains.

The upward trajectory of the AM, from primitives to phenomena, is an essential support of its judgment reframing. Section 2 provides an intuitive introduction of the upward perspective. Under this interpretation, a bias is often what a hidden dimension looks like when seen from a projection that lacks it. The model's formalism makes the reframe more than just a metaphor. The arena, its fields, and the operations that act on them translate the metaphor into a derivable consequence of how a person's judgment is confined to a lowerdimensional slice of the arena (Def. 3.3).

An object earns its place in the AM account by compression, or how much of the JDM catalog it collapses, weighed against what it costs to posit. Objects are not admitted for correspondence to any neural or computational structure (made precise in §3). They allow the AM to describe what is computed, and under what constraints, such that cataloged phenomena follow. Accordingly, it makes descriptive claims. Prescriptive applications may follow as downstream consequences. For example, knowing what generates a bias identifies a potential target for intervention. Rationality axioms and similar normative frameworks of idealized behavior enter as external overlays on the machinery for naming deviations.

The AM account is an invitation to anyone interested in gaining insight into the JDM catalog. It invites readers to trace individual entries to common foundational elements. The procedure in Appendix A outlines how to apply the AM. My hope is that readers will run it: take an entry, locate the signal being read and what it is read as, trace it to its primitive(s), and press on the result. I expect it to fail in places, as the opacity of human thought all but guarantees that specific model mechanics will break down. Yet the underlying concept remains sound: build from primitives, establish composites, and generate predictions. This process is a departure from the historical JDM route of proposing a mechanism, developing a manipulation, and reporting a difference. That process leaves null results ambiguous; the approach I take with the AM generates clearly falsifiable predictions ranging from specific gaps in the catalog to the AM itself.

2 The Projection View

2.1 Judgment is systematic motion

The core AM claim is that judgment is systematic motion along recruited dimensions in a multidimensional space, or arena, that decisions range over. It is composed of fields, or quantities that are defined at every point in the space, much like there is a temperature at every point in a room. Human judgment is capacity-limited [65, 14], meaning people cannot consider their judgment arena in its entirety. The AM represents that limitation by requiring judgment to operate on a low-dimensional projection of the arena. A person must establish a working arena, the projection, by selecting a basis, the few dimensions they recruit from the arena, to form judgments.

Researchers face a similar limitation. Empirical constraints force them to measure low-dimensional projections of a person's judgments. Two options that appear identical in the projection they have selected may be genuinely different points in the full judgment arena. A projection is a choice of coordinates, and a coordinate is a dimension together with an origin and a scale. Thus, two options can differ within a projection in three ways -along a dimension the experimenter did not measure, in the position of an origin, or in scale. Observed inconsistencies can become projection artifacts under this claim. To illustrate, consider a circular shadow cast on a wall. The shadow reveals neither the source object (a sphere, a cylinder, or a flat disc?), its exact position relative to the light, nor its absolute scale. Inferring a person's true judgment from an empirical projection is like identifying the object from its shadow. This is the projection view of biases.

Consider framing. The invariance axiom asserts that description-change is identity-preserving. Framing the projected death rate of a disease around the number of people saved, versus the number of deaths, should not alter a judgment [73]. This axiom is a transformation of the decision situation that should leave judgment unchanged. However, if description-change moves judgment -by recruiting a dimension the axiom ignored, or by relocating the origin the outcome is read against -then the axiom is false about the arena, not violated by the system. "90% survival" and "10% mortality" coincide only in an experimenter's projection.

2.2 The limits of the constancy analogy

We perceive familiar objects as having stable, unchanging properties [8]. I perceive an approaching friend as retaining a stable size across changes in distance, viewing angle, and lighting. It is tempting to draw the analogy that rationality axioms are the JDM constancies and biases are their failures. However, this analogy fails because it requires a distal invariant like a property, characteristic, or structure that the judgment process is trying to recover. My friend -I swear I have one -is really out there. My perceptual system inverts an ecologically limited generative model to give meaning to failure: there was a right answer that context either aided or did not. For much of judgment, recovery lacks a distal invariant. A newly minted mug owner's willingnessto-accept is not recovering an invariant fact about the mug, nor is a potential buyer's willingness-to-pay missing one [41]. Judgment-as-estimation is the narrow exception to this interpretation, as it often focuses on real quantities with ground truths. But many catalog entries, such as framing, preference reversal, and intransitivity, follow the rule and are what the projection view dissolves.

2.3 Heuristics and biases survey the arena

The JDM catalog entries are essential empirical anchor points. They lend valuable insight into human behavior, particularly when a decision necessitates understanding population-level tendencies. Moreover, for present purposes, every documented anomaly in the JDM catalog points at the dimension(s) it reveals. Framing, for example, provides charted coordinates in the nominal dimensions of "reference" and "pragmatics" (speaker's emphasis). Under the projection view, those coordinates are a point in the judgment arena where Tversky and Kahneman noticed an intelligible projection. Their result captures one low-dimensional projection of framing: the circle on the wall only supports one reading unless the viewer has reason to ask what cast it. Framing a disease as having a 90% survival rate relies on the interpreter's reference value for what constitutes a good rate while glossing over the speaker's persuasion intentions. The bias emerges from forcing the projection that hides the pragmatics dimension. Asking the question in a different way can reverse the interpretation, unlock the pragmatics dimension, or reveal new dimensions, a famously robust result from self-report studies [62].

2.4 Competing projections

The JDM community already recognizes such movement across collapsed dimensions. Consider the endowment effect, which describes the tendency for people to assign a higher value to things simply because they own them. The classic explanation of the effect is loss aversion: giving up the item feels far more painful than the pleasure of acquiring it [41, 51]. However, this explanation is hotly contested. For example, Plott and Zeiler [56, 57] argued that the gap is an artifact of procedure. They showed that with practice rounds, incentive-compatible elicitation, and anonymity, the disparity and the exchange asymmetry disappeared. The effect also attenuates sharply among experienced traders [47]. Applying the Plott-Zeiler controls to a wider stimulus set showed that the disparity vanished for mugs but persisted for lotteries [35], and a largescale review concluded the phenomenon is robust in aggregate while resisting explanation by any single mechanism [51].

The JDM community recognizes that the endowment effect emerges from the act of sliding along a dimension collapsed by its projection. The dispute, then, is less about whether valuation gaps arise than about what generates them. Each advance researchers make in this debate uncovers new coordinates of the effect, helping the discipline accurately place it in the judgment arena. I interpret it as evidence of the projection view doing work long before it was named. Importantly, those coordinates reveal judgment dimensions for the arena model to capture.

To study the endowment effect, framing, or any other bias, a researcher must select a basis, the truncated set of judgment dimensions. Any observed judgment, under the projection view, is a higher-dimensional process seen by the experimenter as a low-dimensional projection across their chosen dimensions. The same is true for their participants. When faced with a judgment, a person must instantiate their working arena, a similarly low-dimensional projection that is cue-driven from slow, accumulated structure. The endowment effect literature is evidence of researchers iteratively attempting to calibrate their projections to the projections of subjects.

Many biases are found in the delta between experimenter and participant projections. When a participant moves through their working arena during a judgment, misalignment between the participant's and experimenter's projections hides part of the participant's path. When the path trace leaves the experimenter's projection, trying to reconcile it with the assumed basis can result in the path looking unpredictable, even irrational. The original endowment effect experiment worked because the experimental procedure demanded a particular basis from the participants and that basis was misaligned with the hypothesized basis. The conversation that has transpired in the literature since the original publication, when viewed through the projection view lens, is a discussion around aligning those bases. Each iterative pass has altered the endowment effect's coordinates, either by unlocking a new judgment dimension or by showing how the judgment was stable in the experimental basis, but moving elsewhere.

An experimenter's selection of a basis is ordinary dimensionality reduction, which makes an empirical question tractable by supplying a reference for comparison. Here, the term projection refers specifically to that reduction and carries no assumption that unmeasured alternatives interact. This process and the discovered insights provide a firm foundation for deriving a generative model capable of uncovering what may be occurring in the collapsed dimensions of a judgment projection.

2.5 Dimension proliferation risk

The obvious risk of a modeling paradigm that posits dimensions to explain observed variance, versus strictly stating what dimensions are under consideration, is proliferation. Any inconsistency is quickly explained by simply adding another dimension to the model. This was the fault in Ptolemy's stars: whenever astronomers observed celestial bodies moving contrary to what his base model predicted, they simply added more epicycles instead of scrapping the original model [44]. For the AM, proliferation strips it of its utility as a compact, generative tool and forces it to compete with other anomaly-explaining approaches, such as quantum cognition.

Guarding against this risk means treating a dimension and an origin as what they are: two different components of a coordinate (Def. 3.3), each having its own criterion. A dimension earns admission by being independently measurable and doing work across many phenomena; a hidden axis invoked to save a single anomaly, and never checked against any other case, has not met this bar. An origin does not add an axis, only relocates the zero already attached to a recruited one. The AM subjects origins to a narrower discipline while dimensions face the cross-phenomenon requirement above. An origin shift becomes an epicycle when the origin move cannot be traced to something the person plausibly had to compare against (recent experience, a salient anchor, a typical case, or an expected outcome) and is posited only because it rescues the result. Note that scale is different in that pure rescaling leaves judgment unchanged ( §3), excluding it as an explanation for an anomaly.

The rules above discipline how a dimension or origin may be posited. They leave open the separate question of which biases warrant reaching for one in the first place. Not every bias or apparent inconsistency in judgment is mismeasured rational behavior. The projection view addresses a particular class of observed biases characterized by hidden-dimension artifacts; framing and the endowment effect are the standard cases of this class. Their documented inconsistencies, I argue, arise when the experimenter's projection obscures the participant's movement along the hidden dimension(s). The projection view lets us predict and explain this class of biases from the JDM catalog.

At least two additional classes fall outside the projection view. The first consists of capacity and noise limits. As Herbert Simon famously noted, humans are boundedly rational [65]. Our brains have finite processing capacity, information is almost always incomplete, and we frequently face strict time constraints, all of which hinder our ability to fully engage with a judgment.

When a person loses track of their early reasoning steps during a judgment under load, the judgment may not move systematically along an unmeasured dimension. Losing those early steps is an actual failure. As will be made clear, the AM handles this class of failures with machinery for capacity-and decay-imposed limitations. A second class consists of ecological adaptations: behavior suited to environmental statistics but scored against a mismatched standard [27]. Because these are overlay problems, they do not dissolve under the projection view and the account concedes them as outside its purview.

The AM dissolves the hidden-dimension class of biases, handles capacityand noise-based biases via its machinery, and generally concedes that many, such as those explained by ecological adaptations, are outside its purview.

2.6 The projection view is falsifiable

The projection view holds that a judgment relies entirely on the dimensions recruited as its working arena. A transformation affecting an unrecruited dimension will not impact the judgment. This premise lets the theory derive invariances of judgment by identifying conditions under which biases should not appear.

This approach is not the first to derive predictions from theory. For example, evidence supporting the argument that human cognition is best adapted to natural frequencies followed from formal predictions that stated aligning statistical information with such an adaptation would help people solve associated problems [13, 28]. Seen from the AM perspective, a format change represents a coordinate re-encoding within the same dimensional space, and a format effect is not a true invariance. The projection view claim goes beyond boundary conditions to establish dynamics in which judgment remains constant despite underlying dimensional shifts. The arena model generates invariance predictions across the JDM catalog because the core question that it asks -which dimensions constitute the working arena? -is portable to every entry.

The typical process of making a new JDM catalog entry is to propose a mechanism, find a manipulation, and report a difference. That process has produced a legion of valuable behavioral insight; however, ambiguity characterizes its null results. Researchers are left with the task of determining whether a null result was from incorrect operationalization of the theorized mechanism, a lack of power for the manipulation, or the wrong stimulus. In contrast, the projection view shifts its gaze from predicting behavioral effects to invariances. This shift facilitates a different type of behavioral science, but comes with its own demands and risks. First, it requires committing to which dimensions a person recruits during the judgment under scrutiny. Second, a transformation must be identified that leaves what the judgment reads untouched. Shifting a dimension into play or relocating a reference the judgment is read against is not allowed. Section 3 makes those two requirements precise once the necessary machinery has been laid out.

That commitment is the sword that the AM lives and dies by. Because the requirements are stated in terms of the dimensions a person recruits, a failed invariance can always be explained after the fact by asserting that the transformation must have violated one of them. To rule this out, both the recruitment claim and the neutrality of the transformation must be committed to in advance, on grounds independent of the result they are used to predict (the same discipline imposed on dimensions in §2.5). A transformation argued to be neutral only after the invariance failed is not evidence for the account; it is the epicycle the projection view exists to avoid. When a preregistered invariance does fail, the honest reading is that the posited working arena, the posited reference, or the transformation was wrong. The AM account is designed to say where to look first during failure diagnostics.

For a practical illustration, consider the account's separation of attribute framing from valence framing. Highlighting an apartment's air conditioning or its distance from a busy street is pure basis selection. Highlighting one changes which dimensions enter the working arena. Gain-and-loss wording works differently. "Save $100 a month compared to the median rent" and "lose $100 a month by renting elsewhere" invoke the same dimension (monthly cost) but imply different reference points, so the origin moves while the arena stays put. The AM prediction follows directly: a paraphrase that changes neither the dimensions in play nor the implied reference should not have an effect. Wording matters when it recruits a dimension or relocates an origin; otherwise, it does not.

The distinction casts new light on the canonical framing demonstration. One minus survival is mortality, a single dimension described from opposite sides. The manipulation reverses the dimension in play, but critically, reversing an axis relocates its origin to the opposite end. Orientation and origin move together. The original study was not designed to separate such mechanisms, but to demonstrate the framing effect. That being said, the field's most-cited framing result cannot isolate an orientation from an origin manipulation. Testing them requires cases that manipulate one while holding the other fixed.

3 The Arena Model

The arena model has three layers. Primitives are the irreducible components of judgment: the space over which it ranges, the fields defined on that space, and the settings a person brings to it. Composites are assembled from primitives, and are where observed biases originate. Phenomena are the entries of the JDM catalog; they are what we measure, name, and argue over.

Four terms I rely on deserve defining. Primitives generate a signal: they are the machinery that produces it. A readout takes a quantity as input: it operatively depends on that quantity, and only what a readout takes as input can move a judgment. A derivation invokes a quantity when it names that quantity in explaining a phenomenon. It may invoke quantities outside the agent's operative input, including the reading warranted by a standard. An overlay, like a rationality axiom, scores a judgment against an external yardstick. That yardstick remains external to the machinery.

Two pairs also deserve defining. A judgment's warranted signal is the quantity the standard it is scored against would require; its operative signal is the quantity it actually goes on, which is to say the quantity its readout takes as input. The two often coincide (Appendix A). A misreading is then a fact about a trace: the operative signal is taken to report something it does not report. An error is a verdict, returned when a judgment is scored. A misreading gets no verdict in the absence of an applicable standard, and a judgment can be scored an error with no misreading in it, as when capacity truncates a sample.

Primitives earn their keep by compressing an otherwise overwhelming catalog of ideas. Composite-level description remains useful after compression to primitives. Although protons are made of quarks, nuclear chemistry is done in protons. Salience, according to the AM, is a composite like a proton. Behavioral science, like nuclear chemistry, operates at that level. The primitives explain why composites exist and how they can be perturbed, while composites are what we model higher-order phenomena with.

The AM is at Marr's computational level [50]. It specifies what is computed and under what constraints, leaving algorithms and physical realization open. This commitment has three consequences. First, functional role defines each object. Second, accuracy, coherence, and consistency characterize judgment outcomes; the machinery itself implements no normative standard. Third, each object receives a signature and boundary conditions, while algorithmic accounts supply the functional forms.

3.1 The arena and the working arena

Definition 3.1 (Equipped arena). The equipped arena is Σ = ( 𝒳, 𝑤, 𝐷 ), where 𝒳 is the arena, 𝑤 ∶ 𝒳 × 𝒳 → ℝ ≥0 is the cost function, and 𝐷 ∶ 𝒳 → ℝ ≥0 is trace-density. The arena is a product of 𝑁 ordered coordinate axes, each an interval of ℝ, carrying a reference measure with respect to which the integrals below are taken.

The equipped arena sets the stage with the AM's required elements, which are tackled as the build requires. The reference measure is what enables a field to be integrated over the arena. The account's central functionals, the capacity constraint (Def. 3.6) and the entropy of a field (end of this subsection), are of this kind. The coordinates are ordered because a coordinate is a dimension given an origin and a scale (Def. 3.3), and a scale presupposes order. Because coordinates presuppose order, the model represents categorical structure as regions. The product structure makes marginalization, and in consequence projection onto a subset of coordinates, well defined. 𝑁 is finite because the claim that judgment runs in fewer dimensions than the arena is fundamentally a claim about counts.

Over the timescales addressed by the AM, 𝑁 remains fixed. Expertise accumulates trace-density along an existing dimension and thereby changes the basis. Learning to taste tannin separately from acidity in wine illustrates such a change. Fixing 𝑁 prevents the arena from absorbing any anomaly through dimensional expansion. That would conceal a proliferation risk within the model's machinery and undermine my falsifiability commitment ( §2.5).

The equipped arena names what the space carries and the record of where the system has been. On its own, the arena is a collection of points. Equipping it with 𝑤 and 𝐷 provides interpretive structure required for judgment to happen. Σ2 (cost-distance is derived) 𝑑 𝑤 (𝑎, 𝑏) is the infimum, over finite chains 𝑎 = 𝑥 0 , 𝑥 1 , … , 𝑥 𝑘 = 𝑏, of ∑ 𝑗 𝑤(𝑥 𝑗 , 𝑥 𝑗+1 ). Definition 3.2 (Cells and regions).

A cell is the basin of a local mode of 𝐷 under 𝑤: the set of points from which the cheapest 𝐷-increasing transitions terminate at the same mode. The mode is the cell's prototype; 𝑑 𝑤 from a point to that prototype is its typicality. A region is any member of the family generated under intersection by the cells and the cost-balls of Σ2.

Cells are slow structure. They are fixed by 𝑤 and 𝐷 before an episode begins and are independent of the basepoint from which the current judgment is measured (Def. 3.5, below). Their stability within a judgment makes a bare cell a well-formed region. This feature accommodates categorical candidates such as "bank teller" ( §4). Cost-balls, by contrast, are episode structure measured from the current basepoint. Together, they generate regions, whereas either alone is a limiting case.

The AM defines category boundaries topologically to avoid introducing a free cost threshold. A basin boundary falls where 𝑤 redirects the ascent, requiring no fitted cutoff. What the definition forbids is a category cutting across cost structure. Members of a cell are, by construction, mutually cheaper to move among than across its boundary. This extends the two-channel test developed in §5: a single cost function fit to response time and error should reproduce both similarity ratings and category boundaries, from the same data and with no further fitting.

Ad hoc categories ("things to take from a burning house" [5]) are a key test case, as their members are not mutually cheap. The AM's answer is that a goal clamp (the clamp operation, §3.3) induces low-cost structure while it is held, so the cell is formed by the clamp and is paid for in rent. The prediction that follows is that ad hoc categories are capacity-expensive and dissolve on release, whereas common categories are free and persist. This categorical boundary represents a primary structural exposure of the AM.

Definition 3.3 (Working arena).

A judgment instantiates a working arena 𝑋 together with a projection 𝑃 𝑋 ∶ 𝒳 → 𝑋. A dimension is a direction of variation; a coordinate is a dimension made measurable by fixing an origin and a scale. A projection is thus a choice of coordinates, and two projections can differ in exactly these three components -which dimensions are recruited, where their origins sit, and at what scale. Which of these can move a judgment is fixed by what a readout takes as input. As Def. 3.14 settles, dimension and origin can move a judgment, whereas scale cannot.

3.2 Primitives

The primitives fall into two role-based categories: what is happening now, the states; and what the person brings, the settings. Note that the cost function and trace-density, which the arena carries and which were given above as components of the substrate, are also primitives. 1

3.2.1 The states

Definition 3.4 (Possibility field). The possibility field 𝐸 ∶ 𝒳 × 𝑇 × 𝐵 × Π → ℝ ≥0 , written 𝐸(𝑥 | 𝜏, 𝛽, 𝜋): a distribution over states, indexed by their displacement from the agent's own present, where: • 𝜏 ∈ 𝑇 is a temporal index parameterizing past vs. future extension; • 𝛽 ∈ 𝐵 is a branch index parameterizing actual vs. counterfactual alternatives; • 𝜋 ∈ Π is a perspective index parameterizing self vs. other positioning.

The null displacement (now, actual, self) is admissible; 𝐸 at that index is what discrepancy compares against the actuality field ( §3.3). Displacement away from it is what makes the field a field of possibility rather than a second copy of the present.

Boundary conditions.

E1 (total support and normalization) 𝐸 is defined for all 𝑥 ∈ 𝒳 and is normalized at each index: ∫ 𝒳 𝐸(𝑥 | 𝜏, 𝛽, 𝜋) 𝑑𝑥 = 1 for every (𝜏, 𝛽, 𝜋). Horizon is the 𝜏 at which 𝐻[𝐸 𝑋 ] is high enough that a mass readout no longer discriminates candidates above 𝜃 (Def. 3.6).

Extension along decreasing 𝜏 is narrower but reconstructed, and is tethered to 𝐷. E6 (indices are flat) 𝐸 admits one value per index. Nesting an index inside another requires variable binding, which lies above the substrate ( §3.4).

Variations in the concentration functional (E3) define two formal regimes: expectation, characterized by low entropy (𝐻[𝐸]; density concentrated over a narrow region), and anticipation, characterized by high entropy (density distributed over a broad region). Because E2 permits non-zero mass without input support, 𝐸 generates predictions without needing external input. Pattern detection in noise and spontaneous anticipation are structural consequences of this same generative support.

Definition 3.5 (Actuality field).

The actuality field 𝑆 ∶ 𝒳 → ℝ ≥0 : the agent's representation of the current state. S1 (representation, not world)

Because it is point-valued, ball membership, and therefore region membership (Def. 3.2), is crisp. Current-state uncertainty is carried by 𝐻[𝑆] and does not blur which region a content falls in. 3.2.2 The settings

Definition 3.6 (Capacity and activation). Capacity 𝐾 ∈ ℝ + is a scalar reservoir and activation 𝐴 ∶ 𝒳 → ℝ ≥0 its allocation. Candidacy is being above 𝜃; resolution is sampling depth, the number of draws taken before a readout, which is one of the three ways deliberation spends 𝐾 ( §4.4). Maintenance is priced by duration of hold: the instantaneous price is the draw of the region held, ‖𝐴| 𝑅 ‖ 𝐾 , termed rent throughout. Integrated over its duration, rent is capacity-time committed to the held region and unavailable elsewhere, against a fixed 𝐾. Rent is measured in capacity units; valence is a separate kind of reading. K5 (decay) Unsupported 𝐴 decays toward 𝐷. Content between 𝐷 and 𝜃 remains elevated and decaying while drawing zero capacity from 𝐾.

Heuristics, under this account, are a consequence of capacity pricing. If computation were free, judgment could always derive an analytical solution and would never substitute a faster or more frugal process.

Definition 3.7 (Drive-gain). The drive-gain field 𝑔 assigns to each direction of 𝒳 a signed gain that may vary with position along that direction: 𝑔 = (𝑔 1 , … , 𝑔 𝑁 ), where each 𝑔 𝑖 ∶ ℝ → ℝ takes the position 𝑥 𝑖 along coordinate 𝑖 to a signed gain 𝑔 𝑖 (𝑥 𝑖 ). 𝑔 𝑋 denotes its restriction to the recruited coordinates of a working arena. A constant 𝑔 𝑖 is the ordinary case and the one most of the account operates in; the field form is what permits a gain to reverse across a dimension (G2). The scalar 𝛾 that sets recruitment breadth (X3) is not a component of 𝑔: it governs how many coordinates are recruited, whereas 𝑔 weights what is read on the ones that are.

Boundary conditions.

G1 (sign is primitive) sign(𝑔 𝑖 ) is not derivable from 𝐸, which is descriptive; from unsigned magnitude, which cannot flip; or from comparison, which yields sign only relative to a standard.

G2 (sign boundaries)

A sign boundary is a position 𝑥 * 𝑖 at which 𝑔 𝑖 changes sign. Where mass sits at or across such a boundary, the contribution of coordinate 𝑖 to the valence profile (Def. 3.14) does not pass smoothly through zero: concentrated mass crossing 𝑥 * 𝑖 reverses that contribution, while distributed mass yields a reading that is continuous but non-differentiable at 𝑥 * 𝑖 . Sharp reversal and a kink are thus the same fact at two levels of concentration, not two mechanisms. Drive-gain is a sign-carrying dispositional setting, the irreducible protovalue that attaches to a dimension, and the quantity states can be more or less of. Two quantities are read off it: stakes (G3) and valence (Def. 3.14). Read out over candidates, valence becomes value in the ordinary and economic sense. The two words name one quantity; valence the signed reading, value what that reading is taken to be. Scale is distinct from gain: scale is the unit of a coordinate and multiplies positions, whereas gain multiplies contributions to a readout. So, two people may share a coordinate system and differ entirely in gain.

G3 (readouts)

When an origin and a scale are selected for a dimension, it becomes a coordinate. Because a coordinate requires an origin and a scale, every coordinate is ordered. Categorical structure enters the account as regions, specifically cells crossed with cost-balls (S6). Coordinates are constructed for each judgment by sampling slow structure under a cue.

From this follow two distinct focus-narrowing effects, acting at different stages. Elevated 𝛾 concentrates recruitment over fewer dimensions when the working arena is spawned, capturing physiological arousal narrowing breadth (X3). Once the arena is fixed, high stakes pull the capacity budget toward gainweighted coordinates, which is a second narrowing, for a different reason and within a basis already set. That ordering ensures the account stays non-circular: because stakes is defined on the already-recruited coordinates (G3), it can drive allocation without helping to choose them. Because 𝛾 and ‖ ḡ‖ dissociate (G4), the two narrowings do not need to coincide: the model predicts that an arousal manipulation, such as caffeine, narrows without raising stakes; a calm expert carries stakes without arousal narrowing. The classic arousal-narrowing result [19] is the 𝛾 route.

3.3 Operations

Eight operations act on the fields defined above. Two are distinguished by a load-bearing difference in arity: compare takes a candidate and a standard, whereas coherence takes a set of arbitrary size. The structural limitations of compare warrant the 𝑚-ary form of coherence.

Definition 3.8 (Compare-to-standard). compare 𝑖 ∶ (candidate, standard) → ℝ, returning the signed difference along coordinate 𝑖 between the candidate and the standard. Each operand is read on 𝑖 as a position: a point-valued operand supplies its own, and a field-valued operand supplies the first moment of its marginal on 𝑖, which is the same reading valence takes (Def. 3.14). The standard is that coordinate's origin (X1), except in discrepancy, in which case the standard is the actuality field. Discrepancy is the case compare 𝑖 (𝐸(⋅ | now, actual, self), 𝑆). The predictiveprocessing and reinforcement-learning literatures call this case "prediction error" [23, 15]. I use discrepancy throughout, because prediction is one regime of the possibility field, not a separate faculty. The simultaneous requirement for two operands forces Definition 3.5. Because compare is indexed, discrepancy is a profile over the recruited coordinates, not a single number. When a scalar is wanted, as in salience recruitment ( §3.7), it is the norm of that profile. The same holds of progress (Def. 3.15), which is compare against a clamped region.

Definition 3.9 (Coherence). coherence ∶ {𝑥 1 , … , 𝑥 𝑚 } → ℝ, returning a scalar fit over a set of co-active contents. It takes no standard, is symmetric under permutation, is not defined relative to a dimension, and its value is unsigned.

Compare-to-standard returns a signed difference along a single coordinate, so it represents the relative positions of a candidate and standard on that axis. That functionality makes dissonance unreachable for it. "I am honest" and "I lied" are not incommensurable because both concern the self and honesty. The compare operator would happily place them at different points on an honesty axis, losing information about how the latter is evidence against the former. The coherence operation captures this relation by evaluating a set of arbitrary size and returning an unsigned fit independently of any dimension or standard.

Definition 3.10 (Gate). The coupling from input to 𝑆. Open: 𝑆 tracks input. Closed: 𝑆 is decoupled from input and writable by 𝐸.

Definition 3.11 (Clamp). clamp ∶ (𝐸, 𝑅) → 𝐸, the restriction of 𝐸 to a region 𝑅, either input-driven (via the gate, not under allocation control) or allocationdriven (held by 𝐴, and so drawing rent ‖𝐴| 𝑅 ‖ 𝐾 for as long as it is held). An admissible clamp satisfies 𝐻[clamp (𝐸, 𝑅)] ≤ 𝐻 [𝐸].

The entropy condition is a constraint on admissible forms, not a theorem. Conditioning on a specific region can actually increase entropy, as when a sharply peaked field is restricted to a broad, flat region. Only the average over conditioning events is guaranteed to be non-increasing. Requiring the inequality is what makes clamping the entropy-reducing direction, against which update (Def. 3.12) is the entropy-permitting one.

Definition 3.12 (Update). On discrepancy, 𝐸 moves toward 𝑆 with precisionweighted gain. Direction is opposite to clamping, meaning entropy may increase. Semantic memory is 𝐷 read directly, without a temporal index; episodic memory is the possibility field at a located past index, tethered to 𝐷. Retrieval and the construction of a working arena are the same operation at different levels: both are sampling over slow structure.

Definition 3.14 (Readout). Readouts are taken on the working arena, of the pushed-forward field 𝐸 𝑋 . Two types are distinguished by what they take as input.

A probability readout is the mass of 𝐸 𝑋 over a region, and needs the field's shape. A valence readout is a profile over the recruited coordinates, and needs its first moment: for each recruited coordinate 𝑖, where 𝑜 𝑖 is the origin supplied by one of the four sources of X1 and 𝐸 𝑖 is the marginal of 𝐸 𝑋 on 𝑖.

If 𝑔 𝑖 is constant over supp 𝐸 𝑖 (the ordinary case) this reduces to (𝜇 𝑖 -𝑜 𝑖 ) 𝑔 𝑖 with 𝜇 = 𝔼 𝐸 𝑋 [𝑥].

A derivation requiring a single signed reading uses a scalar combination over the profile. Which combination is an algorithmic-level question and left open ( §3.6). The AM commits to per-coordinate gain-weighted displacement, not to any rule for collapsing it.

For any magnitude readout, a compressive warp applies to the profile after the weighting, not inside it. Thus curvature in a value function comes from the warp, while any kink comes from a sign boundary in 𝑔 (G2), allowing them to be manipulated apart. Source attribution (that something really happened) is itself a readout, thus fallible [37].

Because a readout takes as input the pushed-forward field together with the origin compare reads against, both must be held fixed for a judgment to be unchanged. A transformation Φ is judgment-neutral when it satisfies both of and Φ leaves the active origin unchanged.

The first stipulation says the transformation may move mass anywhere it likes among the collapsed dimensions, so long as nothing changes in the marginal the judgment actually reads. The second says it must not relocate the standard. The AM predicts that a judgment-neutral transformation has no effect whatsoever, and that any transformation failing either stipulation may have one. This is the formal statement of the invariance requirement introduced in §2.6. The whole marginal must be preserved, not just its first moment. If a gain reverses across a dimension (G2), two distributions with the same mean and different shape read differently, so moment-matching is not sufficient. The two stipulations are what the two kinds of readout take as input: a probability readout reads the marginal alone, so the first conjunct governs it; a valence readout reads the marginal against the origin (Def. 3.14), so it needs the second as well. The pair is sufficient because nothing a readout takes as input is left free.

A coordinate carries a third component, its scale, but the scale's form is its constraint. A pure rescaling leaves both quantities unchanged, since 𝑔 𝑖 carries inverse units and a region rescales with its coordinate. Scale-invariance is a consequence of the condition, not an addition to it.

Definition 3.15 (Goal). goal ∶ (𝑅, 𝜏 > now) → clamp (𝐸, 𝑅) held at that index by 𝐴 with 𝑔 ≠ 0 somewhere on 𝑅, and drawing rent for as long as it is held. A goal is therefore an allocation-driven clamp; a point clamp is a desire, a path clamp is an intention. Subgoals are intermediate states on low-cost paths, reached by backward spread along 𝑤, so goal hierarchy is encoded by path structure. Progress is compare 𝑖 (clamp (𝐸, 𝑅), 𝑆). Worth is the valence readout taken on the clamped region (Def. 3.14), whereas for a path clamp it is taken at the endpoint, since a route and its destination are worth the same and cost differently.

Competition, not threshold, releases a goal. Threshold applies to allocationdriven clamps only. Rent is a capacity quantity and worth is a valence reading, so they are not directly comparable (capacity forces an ordering). When a competing demand cannot be funded, a held clamp is released. The one released is always the cheapest to lose, which is the maintenance analog of the local stopping rule on traversal that §3.5 already licenses. That ordering, which clamp is cheapest to lose, is a computational-level commitment. How each clamp's worth (a profile, Def. 3.14) is collapsed to make the comparison is an algorithmic-level choice left open ( §3.6). An input-driven clamp has a different fate: it is not held by 𝐴, draws no rent, and lapses with the input that set it. An implication is that an externally imposed clamp cannot be budgeted away, so the two types come apart under load, such that an intrusive percept persists while a maintained goal is shed.

Two further consequences distinguish competition from a threshold rule. A goal that has stopped paying does not lapse on its own schedule; it persists and intrudes until something else needs the capacity, which is the Zeigarnik pattern ( §4.4 and following). Because rent scales with the region held while worth reads the endpoint, an intention under load degrades to a desire (the route is dropped, the destination kept) instead of being abandoned outright.

Because 𝐾 is treated as static over the episode (the timescale this account addresses), release runs only by competition. A goal held in the absence of another one contending for the capacity has no route to release, which means that the account cannot drop a hopeless goal in a genuinely empty field. This is the cost of the static-capacity bracket. A depleting reserve would supply the internal adjustment beyond affordability needed to model commitments that persist past the point at which maintaining them stops paying. I view that as a step up in modeling to the algorithmic level that belongs to an account of decision making, which can discipline the depletion and recovery rates against persistence data.

3.4 The ceiling

The substrate expresses values over a space. Anything requiring a bound variable, or an argument that can be filled by different things, lies above it. A bound variable is a slot, not a fact, and people will fill "what (blank) knows" with different, sometimes conflicting, content. Reasoning about that content requires siloing the unique content of different people, including different versions of oneself. The substrate represents content in a common space. Maintaining your knowledge and somebody else's as sealed, conflicting states requires rolebased silos above that substrate. Embedded attitudes, exact cardinality, nested quantification, relational and analogical mapping, and theory of mind lie above the AM. The model specifies the interface to this layer and leaves its internal operations open. Theory of mind can help illustrate the split: pointing the perspective index at another agent gives a shallow view of how it looks from there, which is something the arena can support. Holding that agent's knowledge sealed off from one's own means maintaining a conflicting state nested inside the other's viewpoint. That is a bound-variable operation (E6), and lies above the ceiling.

3.5 Governing dynamics

Local processes govern the AM. Homeostasis sets 𝑔 over time (G5), resourcerationality motivates a local stopping rule on traversal, and coherence exerts local pressure over co-active contents [46]. Imposing any of these as a global objective would invert the direction of explanation on which the account depends.

3.6 Algorithmic details left open

The AM leaves a roster of functional forms open: the decay rate of activation, the sampling procedure in retrieval, the functional selecting the basepoint from 𝑆 𝑋 , the shape of the readout warp, the cost of maintaining a clamp, the learning rate on 𝑤, and the combination procedure at choice. Evidence bearing on the last is often read as impacting accounts like the AM. Whether a person integrates attributes compensatorily or scans them lexicographically [55, 69] is an algorithmic question. Evidence of strategy-switching under load [53, 54] constrains the choice of algorithm. These open forms are algorithmic-level details left to later specification by design.

3.7 The composites

Between the primitives and the catalog entries sit composites, including constructed percepts like salience, value, fluency, felt-effort, felt-uncertainty, and surprise, among others. They are derived but standing, meaning they recur across phenomena, are measurable as units, and are always assembled the same way. Salience, the illustrating case, is activation allocated over 𝒳 by two dissociable recruiters, not read off the stimulus [2]. Section 4.1 derives salience in full and contrasts it with value-distorting accounts. This comparison is interesting because, for the AM, value is constructed for each judgment, never a retrieved preference ( §4.6). It is the possibility field read out under drive-gain weighting, computed on demand and never stored.

The composite layer produces one recurring misreading: a middle-layer signal is taken to report a property of the world. Retrieval fluency read as frequency yields availability. The felt-closeness of an option read as its probability yields the conjunction fallacy. The value percept read as a property of the object yields intrinsic value. Trace-density read as truth yields the illusory truth effect [34].

3.8 The phenomena

Phenomena, the JDM catalog entries, are found at the top AM layer. They are the measurable, replicable regularities that are the arena model's target of explanation. A phenomenon is derived when it follows from primitives plus operations without needing additional machinery. They exist naturally at the top of the upward path that starts at the primitives and passes through the composites. Some phenomena are direct copies of a composite, some are a composite misread as a property of the world (the template of §3.7), and still others fall out of the primitives and operations directly, with no standing composite in between.

The projection view gives the AM its primary reach: phenomena characterized by hidden-dimension artifacts. Phenomena arising from capacity or noise, along with ecological adaptations scored against a mismatched standard, lie outside that view. The AM's machinery can nevertheless handle some of them without dissolving them.

4 Derived, Dissolved, and Handled Results

The arena model retrodicts a considerable portion of the JDM catalog. The subset presented here serves as a consistency check. Each entry provides a sufficient derivation; some admit multiple derivations using the same machinery. When more than one process produces the same observed error, they typically disagree about what would make it disappear. Experiments can test that disagreement, and the differing mechanisms allow an intervention to work in one case but not another.

I selected the cases because they are catalog entries that illustrate how the theoretical account alters interpretation. My derivations follow the procedure in Appendix A. Each example locates two signals (the quantity the judgment warranted and the quantity it went on), identifies the worldly property that second signal is taken to report, and names the primitives that generate it. Catalog entries not covered here can be located by the same procedure.

Dissolved, derived as misreading, handled, and resistant are the four core classifications made by the AM. An entry is dissolved when judgment moves systematically along a hidden dimension, making the apparent error an artifact of the experimenter's basis. The behavior is systematic within the full arena. Other entries are derived as genuine, systematic misreadings during judgment: the operative signal is taken to report something it does not. In those cases, the misreading is a fact about the path trace, and an applied standard supplies the error verdict. Handled entries are real limits of cognition that the AM explains directly. Resistant entries require an operation above the ceiling ( §3.4) and are handed off to another process.

4.1 A composite: Salience

Salience is a ubiquitous, recurring percept that biases attribution and judgment [68]. Stepping through its derivation is good practice before engaging a canonical catalog entry. The AM derives salience as the output of attention allocation: activation (𝐴) allocated over 𝒳, recruited by two dissociable inputs. Stakes, the 𝐸-weighted drive-gain magnitude ‖ ḡ‖, captures how much is at issue -the movement in the grass when it might be a snake. Precision demand draws activation to where the possibility field is inadequate: either because it is diffuse (high 𝐻[𝐸]), like a sound in the dark whose source is unplaced, or because its precision was just disconfirmed (high discrepancy), like a step that is not where the foot expected it. When those two quantities are read as world-signals instead of being routed to attention, they become felt-uncertainty and surprise, standing composites of §3.7, thus attentional siblings to salience.

The derivation places salience in the structural class of entries: it is a composite between the fundamental judgment primitives and the proper phenomena of the catalog. Importantly, salience and value dissociate because stakes recruit salience independently of value (G3). This accommodates rumination, in which attention dwells on uncertainty while value remains constant.

The prominent behavioral economics view treats salience as attribute-level and value-distorting [7]: salient outcomes receive greater weight in valuation. In contrast, the AM locates salience in attentional candidacy, which is independent of value by construction.

4.2 Canonical derivations

Two shapes of derivation appear below. In template cases, a signal internal to the agent is read as if it were a property of the world, so the derivation names the composite being misread. In direct cases, the substrate's own geometry surfaces, and the derivation names the substrate quantity itself. The conjunction and availability examples are of the first kind, similarity asymmetry the second.

Conjunction fallacy.

The classic example asks a person which of two options is more likely to be true: Linda is a bank teller or Linda is a bank teller and a feminist [71]. The compelling result is that people tend to select the latter, despite it representing a subset of the former. The AM derivation of the conjunction fallacy is straightforward: 1.

Locate the signal. A probability judgment reads 𝐸's mass over a region (Def. 3.14). The candidates are regions, not points: 𝑅 𝑇 (bank teller) and 𝑅 𝑇∧𝐹 (bank teller and feminist), with 𝑅 𝑇∧𝐹 ⊂ 𝑅 𝑇 . Read as mass, regional containment strictly dictates the direction of the inequality (∫ 𝑅 𝑇∧𝐹 𝐸 ≤ ∫ 𝑅 𝑇 𝐸), so no conjunction can be judged more probable than its constituent conjuncts. That is the warranted quantity, but it is not the operative one. The operative signal is proximity: the per-candidate felt-closeness of Linda to a representative point, which is her typicality for that category, a readout of the cost-distance 𝑑 𝑤.

2.

Identify what it is read as. Each candidate is instead collapsed to a single representative point, which is the prototype of its cell (Def. 3.2).

Collapsing a region to a point discards how much of the space it covers.

That size is precisely what made the conjunction rule hold. Adding "feminist" moves the representative point closer to Linda, so proximity rises even as the required probability falls. The misreading is not a noisier probability but a different quantity: proximity to a point substituted for the required mass over a region. In other words, attribute-substitution [40] occurs because the mass of 𝐸 collapses to points.

3. Trace its primitives.

The correct reading is a readout of the possibility field 𝐸. The substituted reading is a warped readout of 𝑑 𝑤 , itself derived from the cost function 𝑤 (boundary condition Σ2), and the region-to-point collapse selects each candidate's representative point. The substitution is cheap because 𝑑 𝑤 to a point is directly available, whereas mass over a region must be integrated.

Interventions that off-load the expensive readout, such as formatting information in a frequency format [13, 28], are effective because they make reading the regional mass of 𝐸 cheaper.

The same coupling produces conversion errors in which "all A are B" is read as "all B are A." The directly available reading is the symmetric overlap of the two categories, but the asymmetric relation imposed by the AM would require traversing 𝑤 in the costly direction. As in the conjunction, a cheap readout displaces an expensive one. The difference is only in what gets collapsed: extent there versus direction here. This aligns with mental modeling literature's claim that conversion errors rise under working-memory load [4, 38].

Availability. People judge the frequency or probability of an event by how readily instances come to mind [72].

1. Locate the signal.

A likelihood judgment should read 𝐸's mass over the region of instances (Def. 3.14), the same readout the conjunction requires. The signal actually read is fluency: the felt ease with which instances come to mind.

2. Identify what it is read as.

Fluency is a standing composite, not the cost itself. It is a compressive readout of retrieval cost, so that what is cheap to reach feels fluent. It differs from true frequency: a single recent or vivid instance is highly fluent though rare. Read as frequency, fluency inflates the estimate exactly when ease and frequency diverge.

3. Trace its primitives.

Fluency is a warped readout of retrieval cost, that is, inverse traversal cost over 𝑤, with retrieval sampling along 𝑤 and tethered to 𝐷 (Def. 3.13). The correct reading is a readout of 𝐸; the substitution is cheap because fluency is directly felt, whereas mass over a region must be integrated.

The availability heuristic posits a mental shortcut. The derivation is of the mechanism (ease of retrieval is what gets read when mass should have been), not of the shortcut. The judgment is assembled from whatever instances happen to be sampled [67].

Availability also illustrates a case of rival accounts disagreeing in a testable way. The derivation just given treats the effect as a genuine misreading (fluency substituted for mass). It predicts that the estimate can be corrected by making the set of instances explicit so that mass is read where fluency stood in.

The alternative account claims that there is no error: in natural settings, ease of retrieval tracks frequency, meaning reading one for the other is well calibrated, and the discrepancy surfaces only when a task decouples them. This interpretation belongs to the ecological adaptations class of results that §2.5 sets outside of what the AM resolves. The successor representation account makes the ecological route precise such that each state is encoded by its expected future occupancy, directly coupling accessibility and frequency [16, 26].

The two accounts predict different interventions. The misreading account predicts that forcing an explicit reading of the instance set will remove the effect; the ecological account predicts that restoring the correlation between ease and frequency will remove it. This disagreement is a testable consequence of applying the AM to the JDM catalog.

Similarity asymmetry.

People judge "North Korea is similar to China" as truer than "China is similar to North Korea" [70]: the less prominent item is more similar to the prominent one than the reverse. Unlike availability and the conjunction, the warranted quantities and operative signals coincide. Also, nothing is taken to report a property of the world. The signal is read correctly, meaning that the derivation is structural.

Under the account, similarity is a readout of cost-distance. Similarity is decreasing and compressively warped like any other magnitude read out (Def. 3.14). Because the cost function 𝑤 is directed, the induced 𝑑 𝑤 is a quasimetric (boundary condition Σ2: 𝑑 𝑤 (𝑎, 𝑏) ≠ 𝑑 𝑤 (𝑏, 𝑎), in general). "𝑎 is similar to 𝑏" reads 𝑑 𝑋 𝑤 (𝑎, 𝑏), the cost of reaching 𝑏 from 𝑎 on the working arena, and then reads it inversely, with what is cheap to reach being judged similar. A prominent, high-𝐷 referent is cheap to reach from many starting points, while the reverse traversal is not, so the two phrasings read two different costs.

Another consequence of taking the readout on 𝑋 is that similarity shifts with the coordinates recruited. That shifting accounts for the context-dependence and asymmetry Tversky famously documented [70]. Independent evidence for this form of similarity comes from the successor representation [16, 26], in which experience-dependent learning of directed transitions causes a peripheral state to sit closer to a central state than vice versa. The effect is anomalous only against the assumption that similarity must be symmetric. The directedness of 𝑤 is fixed by the possibility field's forward and backward structure (E4, E5), independently of anything about similarity, so the effect is predicted rather than accommodated.

4.3 Five entries, one separately needed property

Generativity, a core feature of the account, means the field can have mass in a region without input support (see E2). This feature makes prediction and anticipation possible. Unconstrained generativity can also produce pattern over-detection, that is, the perception of structure in its absence. At least five catalog entries hinge on this same mechanism: • Apophenia: the tendency to perceive meaningful patterns, connections, or intent in unrelated data [12].

• Pareidolia: a specific type of apophenia: seeing or hearing familiar images or sounds in random sensory stimuli [64, 48].

• Clustering illusion: perceiving a pattern in a small sample drawn from a random distribution [29].

• Hot-hand fallacy: believing a streak of success raises the odds of further success on the next attempt [30].

• Texas sharpshooter fallacy: treating a cluster found after the fact as if it were a pattern predicted in advance [31].

The AM claims that these, along with anticipation and prediction, are all products of generativity, not rival faculties.

4.4 Dual-process collapses into a spectrum

The dual-process account of cognition [20], colloquially "thinking fast and slow" [39], is one of the most recognizable JDM meta phenomena. Its fast, intuitive process operates with little or no conscious effort or sense of voluntary control. Its contrasting slow, deliberative process handles effortful and conscious mental operations. The core determinants of the fast or slow trade-off map directly to AM components: cognitive load, stakes, and framing map to capacity availability (𝐾 minus current ‖𝐴‖ 𝐾 ), norm of the drive-gain vector (‖ ḡ‖; G3), and the working arena recruitment (𝑋).

The AM treats fast processing as the default because gain limits traversal through a local resource-rational stopping rule. Slow processing draws on capacity in three ways: deeper sampling before readout, more coordinates maintained at once, and longer clamp chains (derivations). These draws are separately measurable and may dissociate. A single quantity that made them covary would restore the "fast and slow" dichotomy and falsify the AM's decomposition. The account aligns more closely with judgment-as-a-spectrum [52] and bounded-rationality [65] accounts.

4.5 A handled limit: the Zeigarnik effect

The AM classifies some catalog entries as handled: real cognitive costs that its machinery explains directly. An uncompleted goal is a region of 𝐸 clamped at a future index and maintained at rent against capacity (Def. 3.15). While the clamp holds, it continues to draw on 𝐾, so interrupted tasks remain accessible and intrude, producing the Zeigarnik effect [75]. Completion releases the clamp, after which the elevated accessibility decays. Where Lewin posited a tension that holds the goal active, the AM derives its persistence from maintenance rent under the capacity constraint.

4.6 Intrinsic value is derived

The AM claims that value is read-out valence, much like decision by sampling [67] and query theory [36]. In contrast, many choice models represent preferences with a utility function over outcomes. For the arena model, value is a percept. It is found in the middle layer, computed on demand, and personrelative. When it is read as a property of an object, it becomes "intrinsic value." This process amounts to naive realism about the value readout, making it a real, derived misreading during judgment. The same move appeared in §2, where a rationality yardstick was treated as a property of the world. This misreading occurs whenever a constructed, agent-relative readout is treated as a recov-erable fact. Recognizing the value readout as agent-relative makes intrinsic value subjective. Because each judgment constructs value anew, preference instability can arise across judgments.

4.7 At the boundary: Curse of knowledge and hindsight bias

The substrate does not support a bound variable ( §3.4). Estimating what another person, or your past self, knows means filling that "knower" slot with someone whose knowledge differs from yours and keeping it quarantined. Also, the substrate is not sealed, so your current knowledge can leak in. Consider the curse of knowledge: a better-informed person is unable to disregard or "un-know" their private information when predicting the mental state of another [11]. Their own knowledge leaks into their representation of the lessinformed person's. Similarly, the hindsight bias happens when an individual falsely overestimates, in retrospect, how predictable an outcome was before it occurred [21]. In its case, present knowledge leaks across time into a person's representation of their past self. The two differ only in which index carries the displacement, the perspective index 𝜋 for another person, the temporal index 𝜏 for a past self. However, both need the sealing operation the flat indices (E6) cannot provide. The possibility field can accommodate another viewpoint, but it cannot nest and seal inside that viewpoint a knowledge state different from one's own. Performing that seal is a bound-variable operation, and that lies above the ceiling.

Half of each bias directly follows from this constraint. With no seal, one's current knowledge leaks into the attempted simulation, whether across persons or time. Per the AM, the self-to-other flow is why the error always runs toward what one already knows, and grows under load. The other half does not follow from the constraint: performing the seal is the bound-variable operation itself, and there is no route to it within the AM. The judgment process can only hand off to a theory-of-mind mechanism above the ceiling [32]. Because the task is to model another mind, that hand-off is constitutive, not optional. The AM specifies the interface at which it occurs. The trigger is an algorithmic question for future work.

The entry is resistant for a stated structural reason: the path trace reaches the ceiling in need of the sealing operation. This is a declared limit of the AM.

A defect would instead arise from absorbing the entry by retrofit or leaving an inconsistency unexplained by either a hidden dimension or a capacity cost. Curse of knowledge exposes this boundary and makes the ceiling empirically meaningful.

5 Predictions

The previous section was a consistency check covering the account's ability to derive, dissolve, and handle existing entries in the JDM catalog. This section demonstrates the account's generative capacity through invariance predictions.

Traditional JDM studies propose a mechanism, find a manipulation, and report a difference, leaving null results ambiguous ( §2.6). An invariance prediction sets a stricter criterion: an observed difference when none should exist undermines the account. Gigerenzer and Hoffrage's frequency-format result for Bayesian reasoning provides a precedent for such a theory-derived null prediction [28]. The AM extends this strategy across the catalog by asking of every entry, "what dimensions does the working arena recruit?" Its central prediction is that a transformation along an unrecruited dimension leaves judgment unchanged.

5.1 The general form of invariances

Recall that the working arena (𝑋, Def. 3.3) is spanned by the dimensions a person recruits during a given judgment. A readout takes as input the possibility field as it appears on that arena together with the origin that the compare operation reads against. A judgment-neutral transformation preserves both. New information confined to an unrecruited dimension moves mass along a collapsed coordinate while preserving the read marginal and active origin, so judgment remains constant. A paraphrase that preserves the recruited basis and active origin has the same result.

By identifying effect-free transformations, this prediction exposes the account to rebuttal. Boundary conditions X1-X3 specify the permitted dependencies of recruitment. An observed difference under a transformation committed to in advance as judgment-neutral falsifies one of three things: the recruitment claim, the claim about which origin is active, or the neutrality of the transformation. These commitments must be named in advance and remain fixed after the result is observed.

The framing example from §2.6 makes this concrete. The account separates dimension framing (choosing which features to discuss) from valence framing (viewing a single feature as a gain or loss relative to a baseline). Famous demonstrations, such as survival versus mortality rates, conflate the two mechanisms: reversing the dimension under evaluation automatically shifts its origin, leaving their contributions to the observed effect confounded. The AM adds a prediction for shifts along dimensions outside the working arena: such shifts produce no framing effect.

5.2 Deliberation dissection

The AM dissects deliberation into three separable draws on a single budget: sampling depth, coordinate breadth, and derivation length ( §4.4). This decomposition differs from unidimensional accounts of a deliberative "system-2" and general-intelligence treatments of deliberative ability [20, 66]. Because the three draws share one budget, some covariation under load is expected. Their independence nevertheless predicts differential sensitivity: each targeted load should primarily impair a different draw. A deadline truncates sampling from 𝐷 before readout (Def. 3.13). A concurrent capacity load pushes against the ‖𝐴‖ 𝐾 ≤ 𝐾 bound and reduces the number of coordinates maintained (K2, Def. 3.6; [3, 14]). An interruption collapses intermediate subgoal steps held at rent (K3, Def. 3.15). Crossing the three manipulations with the three draws produces a 3 × 3 design in which the primary impairment should differ by condition. A single scalar measure of how "slow" a judgment was cannot capture that pattern.

5.3 Identity crowding-out

An identity, such as scientist or cyclist, is not a recruited dimension but a cell (Def. 3.2), that is, a category with a prototype, which enters the account as a region. Making an identity salient places an allocation-driven clamp on that region (Def. 3.11), which draws maintenance rent ‖𝐴| 𝑅 ‖ 𝐾 for as long as it is held (K3). The AM already uses this mechanism for an uncompleted goal and the Zeigarnik effect [75]: a goal is an allocation-driven clamp (Def. 3.15), and a held clamp is capacity spent, hence capacity unavailable elsewhere. Membership effects are, accordingly, cue-recruited (the clamp is set by a cue), trace-densitydependent (the cell is a mode of 𝐷, Def. 3.2), and capacity-limited (the clamp draws on 𝐾).

Because 𝐾 is a single scalar reservoir (K4), an active identity never "lives rent-free." It draws rent in competition with every other demand on 𝐾, such as another salient identity, a maintained goal, or any of deliberation's draws (sampling depth, coordinate breadth, and derivation length; §4.4). The formalism produces two related predictions. First, suppression is graded: rent scales with the region held, so a broader or more strongly maintained identity suppresses concurrent demands more. Second, suppression is reversible: competition sheds a clamp when a rival demand exceeds the available capacity, and releasing the clamp returns that capacity in full under the fixed 𝐾 (K3). Priming an identity should degrade an unrelated concurrent task measurably and reversibly, in proportion to the rent its region commands.

The shared budget applies equally to social and non-social content, making cross-domain crowding-out diagnostic. If priming "scientist" and priming "cyclist" draw equal rent, they should impair a third, unrelated task equally; if one region commands more rent, it should impair that task more. Crowding-out confined to identity-related tasks would undermine the shared-budget claim.

This mechanism has a direct empirical precedent. Priming a self-relevant negative stereotype is shown to reduce working-memory capacity, and the reduction is shown to mediate stereotype threat effects on performance [60]. The AM interprets that as a maintained, self-relevant identity concern drawing on a shared resource and thereby degrading an unrelated task. That is the clamp-rent prediction of this section shown in existing data. 2

5.4 Choice tracks drive-gain, not attribute magnitude

Choice tracks the drive-gain-weighted profile, not raw attribute magnitude. When a dimension's gain is near zero, valence barely responds no matter how large the attribute grows (G3). Consequently, only an intervention that changes the allocation of drive-gain weight will flip choice. Two manipulations emerge from this feature of the AM. First, hold 𝑔 (see Def. 3.7, G3) fixed and inflate an attribute on a gain-gated dimension (one identified as low-gain in advance by the stakes reading (G3), not by the null it is about to produce) as far as you like. The AM predicts choice will not change, a property that holds regardless of the algorithmic implementation because a gated contribution stays negligible under any combination rule. Second, hold the attribute values fixed, but shift 𝑔 by manipulating, for example, satiety. The AM predicts that choice will flip. The first manipulation targets the world (attribute magnitude), whereas the second targets the agent (drive-gain).

To ground this prediction, hold a snack fixed and inflate its rating on a nondominant dimension, such as the spiciness of a tortilla chip. Choice should remain unchanged. Holding the attributes fixed while manipulating hunger changes 𝑔 and should permit choice to flip. A fixed-parameter weighted sum misses this asymmetry because both its weights and attributes remain unchanged. A state-dependent utility model could capture the flip through refitting; the AM locates the state-dependence in 𝑔, a separately measured channel. The paired manipulations address the question of whether 𝑔 governs choice independently of whether the readout is implemented through lexicographic scanning, compensatory integration, or another algorithm.

5.5 Subgoal takeover

A means-state is more easily locked in when the superordinate goal is a highentropy clamp. A diffuse 𝐸 blurs the progress read against the actuality field (E4, Def. 3.15), so discrepancy registers slowly and a concrete subgoal with a crisp progress read draws capacity instead. Temporal distance is the most direct route to that entropy: E4 states that 𝐻[𝐸] grows with 𝜏-distance from the clamp. Abstractness offers a second route. When an abstract goal specifies a broader region of the possibility field (e.g., more states satisfy "be successful" than "submit the draft by 5pm"), 𝐻[𝐸] is raised by support-breadth, not the temporal extension. This second route assumes construal-level abstraction maps onto support-breadth in 𝐸. Because this mapping is not established elsewhere in this account, it is adopted here strictly as a working premise. It also runs counter to the surface reading of the construal literature, on which abstraction strengthens superordinate control [24]; the AM instead bets that takeover tracks the entropy of the goal's target region, not the construal frame itself.

Because the two routes to entropy are independent, they are cleanly dissociable: a proximal-but-abstract goal and a distal-concrete goal both elevate 𝐻[𝐸] through different machinery. Crossing them yields a clean 2 × 2, and if takeover tracks entropy regardless of which route produced it, proximal-abstract and distal-concrete goals should show comparably elevated takeover relative to a proximal-concrete one. The resulting pattern diverges from competing models: a single-route account (in which only temporal distance sets salience) would fail to predict it, as would a fixed hierarchy (which lacks an entropy parameter for either route).

5.6 Two-channel identifiability

A standard psychometric route to fitting a similarity metric is inferring a spatial representation from judged proximities: how similar people say two options feel [63]. Doing that well is straightforward because there is only one number to match. The AM builds the metric differently, out of how costly it is to move from one option to another (𝑤 and the induced 𝑑 𝑤 ). That transition is measured independently, through how long people take and how often they err (Def. 3.1, Σ2). The cost-based metric should then reproduce the similarity ratings on its own with no need to fit it. Agreement across the two measurement channels follows as a falsifiable prediction.

Consider two diagnoses with overlapping symptoms, bacterial pneumonia and viral bronchitis. Under time pressure, a physician might hesitate between them or mix one up with the other. Their response time lags and errors rise whenever a case sits near that particular boundary. Those numbers, collected across many pairs of diagnoses, are what build the cost function 𝑤. Because discrimination difficulty rises with proximity, pairs that are slow and errorprone to separate sit close and are assigned a low cost between them, while pairs the physician never confuses sit far and are assigned a high one. From 𝑤 alone, the model derives the cost-distance 𝑑 𝑤 by chain closure (Σ2), and from 𝑑 𝑤 , a full ranking of how close every diagnosis sits to the others. Separately, with no time pressure, the same physician sorts diagnoses into groups by how similar they seem, a casual similarity judgment, untouched by speed or error.

The AM predicts that the ranking built from triage mistakes should reproduce the groupings built from calm sorting. It is deliberately ordinal: similarity is read through a compressive warp (Def. 3.14), which preserves order while allowing magnitudes to differ. Because the two measures come from independent behaviors, their agreement is an empirical risk. A mismatch between the diagnoses confused under pressure and those grouped together at leisure would falsify the AM; a directly fitted similarity metric does not face this cross-channel test. 3

5.7 Learning relocates but drive-gain does not learn

The dominant account the AM faces here is a two-system one: a goal-directed controller that consults the current value of an outcome, and a habit controller that runs off a stored stimulus-response association [17]. In the reinforcementlearning formulation, the habit system acts on a cached value updated by past reward [15]. The AM assigns experience to position instead. Value is a 𝑔weighted readout taken on demand (Def. 3.14), so there is no cached quantity to go stale. What experience writes is position: relocation in trace-density (𝐷) and cost (𝑤) is how an acquired taste or a practiced action is laid down. 𝑔's role is to process change in those structures, not to change itself (G5). Learning cannot write to 𝑔 by construction. The AM claims that drive state moves 𝑔 on a separate route and timescale, which is what allows for a shift in bodily state to re-value a cue without learning [59], a risky but testable commitment.

Position in 𝐷.

Using mere exposure, build a preference for a novel food, say anchovies on pizza, then satiate on that food's category (salty snacks) and re-test choice. If the acquired preference is position in 𝐷, satiation should devalue the specific food exactly as it devalues any other category member, learned or not, because both are read through the same lowered gain. The opposite is the AM's falsifier: the unlearned preference on that dimension drops while the acquired one holds. The falsifier needs acquisition to write a stored, drive-independent gain forbidden by G5.

Conditioned taste aversion is itself a learned change in a food's worth [25]. On the surface, that reads like learning writing to 𝑔. The AM's interpretation is that aversion relocates mass onto a disgust dimension whose gain is already negative, not a sign-flip on the taste dimension. That means the test above should devalue by satiety, and an aversion-devalued food should show disgustaxis recruitment instead of a reversed taste reading.

Position in 𝑤.

The AM prediction is in the nuance of cost. The basic setup is to use instrumental conditioning to make an action serve a drive (reaching for a water bottle at your desk to stay hydrated), then satiate that drive (drink until no longer thirsty) and re-test choice of the action against an equally practiced alternative serving a different drive (checking email). At moderate training the a direct measure of stable comparison cost [58]. Moment-to-moment response time is shaped by fatigue, a recent cue, and alertness. That momentary state has to be held steady, or accounted for, before the two measurements are truly comparable. Recruitment must match too: because similarity is read on the working arena and shifts with the coordinates recruited, both tasks must recruit the same ones, or a disagreement reflects a shifted arena, not a failed prediction.

AM predicts satiation devalues the reach with thirst, which is what goal-directed responding does [1]. At overtraining the same paradigm yields a response that persists through devaluation [1]. On its surface, that persistence appears to falsify G5.

The AM locates this persistence in recruitment (X1-X3). Overtraining makes the action cue-driven, so the cue recruits the coordinates of the action while leaving the distal dimension served by the outcome collapsed. Thirst is marginalized out by 𝑃 𝑋 . Because valence is read only over the recruited gains (𝑔 𝑋 ; G3, Def. 3.14), a satiation-induced change to 𝑔 on the collapsed coordinate is absent from the judgment. This is the same blindness the general form of invariances turns on: there the unrecruited coordinate carries a mass change; here it carries a gain change. Persistence is a recruitment fact, which is why stress and time pressure, which push 𝛾 up the inverted-U and narrow recruitment (X3), tip behavior toward the habitual pole [61].

This interpretation relocates and sharpens the falsifier. Widen the arena (remove time pressure, reduce arousal, or cue the served drive to recruit thirst (X3)), and the collapsed coordinate re-enters 𝑋. The readout then consults the lowered gain, and devaluation sensitivity returns without requiring new learning. A stored value would not recover: by construction, a cached quantity is value-blind and remains insensitive regardless of how broadly the arena is drawn. The crucial test is whether an overtrained habit persists after the outcome dimension has been recruited. If the reach survives full satiation in an arena that recruits thirst, while its goal-directed counterpart stops, then learning has produced a drive-independent value and falsified G5. The mechanism by which overtraining collapses that coordinate remains an open algorithmic-level question ( §3.6); the computational claim locates persistence in recruitment.

Both routes test one claim, that experience relocates the state and leaves the gain landscape alone. Either failing would independently break G5. 4

6 Discussion

The arena model's small substrate regenerates much of the JDM catalog. It does so by building upward from primitives through to the catalog entries. The form this central result takes is what gives the arena model weight: the catalog's named entries do not each receive their own mechanism, but fall out of one compact set of primitives. The arena model provides the support the projection view requires. From its perspective, much of what JDM research files as irrationality is systematic motion along a dimension an experiment did not measure, or an internal quantity read as if it were a property of the world. A bias, on this account, is not a defect bolted onto reasoning, but the shape a bounded system takes when it reads its own signals.

Two types of evidence support the arena model. Section 4 provides a consistency check by showing how a small primitive set can account for much of the catalog. This establishes the model's capacity to generate the catalog while leaving comparison with alternative accounts of individual entries open. Section 5 demonstrates the central claim that the arena model is a falsifiable predictive tool that stakes itself on invariances and dissociations. The provided examples are currently untested and open to scrutiny.

The arena model relies entirely on existing data; generating new empirical evidence is the objective of the broader research program. Tempting as it is, I refrain from reporting a coverage percentage. Any such figure would reflect subjective selection criteria masquerading as the model's actual reach. Instead, coverage can be verified entry by entry using the procedure in Appendix A, and proportions across the three classes in Section 2.5 are ready for direct measurement, not only rough estimation.

The arena model is intended as a parsimonious account of human judgment, with parsimony assessed through structural dependencies. The primitives divide the catalog according to dependence on the possibility field (𝐸). Availability and the conjunction fallacy operate on fluency and cost-distance; their derivations invoke 𝐸 only to identify the warranted quantity that makes the substitution visible, while the readout remains determined by those operative signals. Similarity asymmetry and conversion errors satisfy the stronger condition of independence from 𝐸 in both operation and derivation. The parsimony claim rests on this stronger condition (Figure 1).

Crucially, the entries that neither take 𝐸 as an input nor invoke it form a clear, principled cluster -not a scatter of exceptions. Magnitude and scaling effects follow directly from the compressive warp (Def. 3.14), relative-encoding effects stem from the directed structure of 𝑤, and similarity effects derive from 𝑑 𝑤 itself. A primitive field invoked universally, without clear boundary conditions, would be unconstrained, absorbing whatever explanatory work is required. By contrast, a field that specific catalog entries provably do not need demonstrates the true economy of a compressed account. Three boundaries keep the account focused and falsifiable. Scope ( §3.4). The substrate expresses values over a space and specifies the interface to higherlevel operations involving bound variables, including nested perspectives, exact cardinality, and relational or analogical mapping. The explicit criterion separating these levels contributes to the AM's falsifiability.

Dynamics ( §3.5). The dynamics directly distinguish the arena model from the free-energy account introduced earlier. Local processes govern the model: homeostasis only sets drive-gain over time (G5), resource-rationality supplies a stopping rule for traversal, and coherence supplies pressure over co-active contents. A free-energy account begins with a global objective and works downward; the arena model begins with primitives and works upward to phenomena.

Level ( §3.6). The account is pitched at Marr's computational level. Several functional forms are intentionally left open for algorithmic specification: the decay rate of activation, the retrieval sampling procedure, the shape of the readout warp, the cost of maintaining a clamp, the learning rate on 𝑤, and the combination procedure at choice.

The account will advance along two axes: downward through Marr's levels, and across the cascade this project opens. At the algorithmic level, open functional forms and choice procedures are empirically resolvable via simulation. A faithful implementation should reproduce the derivations of §4 as emergent results. A central design criterion here is sensitivity. Primitives must couple such that small variations in capacity or gain yield large, structured behavioral divergence. Too little sensitivity will cause artificial convergence; too much will produce pure noise. Finally, the account's primary wager relies on a small, measurable tuning surface restricted to capacity, the gain profile (its shape together with its arousal modulation 𝛾), expertise-driven deviations in 𝑤, and experience-driven deviations in 𝐷. The prediction is that population-typical priors and these few parameters will generate rich behavioral variety through their interaction.

The second axis follows the judgment and decision making trajectory: a model of judgment supports one of decision making, and intern the decision making model will support one of choice. The AM limits supply targets for the next model: thresholding and commitment (how graded readouts become discrete states), response mode as a distinct operation (why one substrate yields a price, choice, or deferral depending on query format), and revision conditions beyond affordability (why commitments persist past the point of return).

Stated together, the predictions in §5 form a falsification register, that is, a list of outcomes that would undermine the arena model. Some are more dangerous than others. The model is at risk if (1) a transformation committed to in advance as leaving the recruited dimensions and active origin untouched nonetheless shifts judgment, breaking the claim that judgment reads only the recruited arena and its origin; (2) the three draws on deliberation prove nondissociable under targeted load, collapsing the decomposition back into the single dichotomy the account explicitly denies; (3) identity crowding-out remains restricted to social tasks, pointing to a dedicated module the account cannot accommodate; (4) choice changes when a gain-gated attribute is inflated with drive-gain held fixed, or fails to flip when drive-gain is shifted with attributes held fixed, breaking the claim that choice tracks drive-gain and not raw attribute magnitude; (5) proximal-abstract and distal-concrete goals fail to show comparably elevated takeover relative to a proximal-concrete goal, undermining the entropy account in favor of a single-route or fixed-hierarchy alternative; (6) the two channels of 𝑤 disagree, breaking the claim that error-speed inferences and direct similarity elicitation measure a single quantity; or (7) a preference learned through either acquisition route resists devaluation when the drive through which it was acquired is satiated, breaking G5's claim that experience relocates the state without writing a drive-independent value. Together, these predictions make the model falsifiable and therefore useful.

7 Conclusion

The Arena Model is an attempt to give structure to the disjointed heuristics and biases catalog. It argues that judgment runs on a low-dimensional projection of a higher-dimensional world. This is the projection view, and it provides a theoretical foundation for a compact model that gives researchers a single, testable account as an alternative to the ever-growing JDM catalog. The procedure in Appendix A is an invitation to apply the AM to catalog entries beyond those covered here. The procedure specifies both the test and its failure condition. The resulting regularities can be read either as a catalog of human error or as the consequences of reasoning with finite resources in an open world.

I hope you find the arena model compelling. Like all models, it is wrong. Like the foundational prior art it builds upon, it is a useful tool for understanding our internal worlds.

A The Locating Procedure

A select few entries are in the body. This appendix gives the procedure to locate any other JDM catalog entry and assign to it what the account says about it, or show that it resists the model. The conjunction fallacy in §4 is this procedure run in full; what follows states it in general.

Step 1 -Locate the signal.

Two quantities are at stake and both are required to locate an entry.

• The warranted signal: the quantity a judgment would have to read for the standard it is scored against to be met. A probability judgment warrants the mass of the possibility field over a region; a value judgment warrants a valence readout.

• The operative signal: the quantity the agent actually uses. This may be a cost-distance 𝑑 𝑤 , a trace-density 𝐷, a felt composite such as fluency, or the warranted quantity itself. A difference between the two signals constitutes a substitution, typically driven by price: the operative signal is directly available, while the warranted one must be integrated. Many familiar entries name, in ordinary language, the signal the agent is using, whereas the warranted quantity typically has to be read off the standard the entry is scored against.

Step 2 -Locate what it is read as.

Identify which property of the world the operative signal is taken to report. Taking the operative signal for a fact about the object rather than a reading taken by an agent constitutes a reification.

The two gaps are distinct, and an entry may carry either, both, or neither.

A substitution is a gap inside Step 1, between what the judgment warranted and what it went on. A reification is a gap between Step 1 and Step 2, between an internal signal and the worldly property it is taken to report.

Where a bias is present, it lives in one of the two.

Step 3 -Trace to primitives.

Identify the primitives and operations that generate the signal. If the path trace closes using machinery already specified, the entry is located. If it closes only by introducing machinery for this entry, that is not a derivation but a retrofit, and should be reported as one. If it does not close at all, proceed to the resistant case below.

Classify the result.

A located entry has so far fallen into one of four kinds, separated chiefly by a single question: is a distal invariant at stake, a right answer that exists independently of how the problem is described?

• Dissolved. No invariant is recovered, and the options judged "inconsistently" differ in a way the experiment did not measure, such as an unmeasured dimension or an origin placed differently. The behavior is systematic motion in the full arena; the inconsistency is a projection artifact. There was no misreading to score.

• Derived as misreading. A signal is read as a quantity it is not. Illustrations include a cheap readout standing in for an expensive one, or an internal quantity taken for a property of the world. There was a misreading, and tracing it says why a sensible system does so.

• Handled. The phenomenon is produced directly by bounded capacity, decay, or readout compression, not by being a cost-driven reading. The distinction is needed because price also explains why a cheap readout displaces an expensive one, so a priced explanation cannot on its own place an entry here. It is explained, but not explained away.

• Resistant. The path trace does not close because the entry requires a bound variable (a nested perspective, a relational mapping, or an exact cardinality), which lies above the ceiling ( §3.4). The account hands off in place of forcing a derivation.

An entry may instead belong to the conceded class of §2.5. In that class, correct behavior is scored against a mismatched standard and the account claims no resolution. Ultimately, the labels are bookkeeping in service of interpretation. Traces are the "deliverables" of the model. Table 2 records examples of these labels applied to the catalog.

A worked case: Framing. Take the framing effect in its canonical form. Six hundred people are threatened by a disease. Their situation can be described either as a number who will be "saved" or a number who will "die." Across those framings, preferences reverse [73]. The principle it is taken to violate is invariance: alternative descriptions of the same outcome should yield the same choice. The relative attractiveness of options, Tversky and Kahneman observe, "varies when the same decision problem is framed in different ways" [73, p. 457]: the same policy for six hundred lives, described as "200 will be saved" or as "400 will die" reverses stated preference.

Run the procedure. Step 1, the signal: the warranted quantity is a valence readout of the described outcome, and it is also the operative one, meaning there is no substitution. Step 2, read as: the two descriptions are taken to be one decision, so the reversal reads as an inconsistency. Step 3, trace: the description sets the origin against which compare reads the outcome (Def. 3.8; the "saved" wording anchors at none-saved, the "die" wording at none-lost), so the two framings are read against different origins, using machinery already in place. Classify: is a distal invariant recovered? No; there is no groundtruth attitude toward the policy the judgment fails to return, so the reversal is systematic motion in the full arena, not an error. The entry is therefore dissolved: invariance is false about the arena, not violated by the system, and the two descriptions coincide only in the experimenter's projection.

Notice that the trace above runs to a relocated origin, which is what a valence readout reads against (Def. 3.14). The canonical stimulus, however, does not let it run there alone. Because one minus survival is mortality, describing the outcome from the other side reverses the dimension in play, and reversing an axis relocates its origin with it ( §2.6). Dimension and origin move together in this case, so this demonstration dissolves the inconsistency without isolating which of the two carries it; separating them requires a stimulus that moves one while holding the other fixed.
