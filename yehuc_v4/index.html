Power analyses need to contain the following information to be interpretable: the type of power analysis, the statistical test, the software used, sample size, critical alpha criterion, power level, effect size, and an effect size metric. In addition, it is recommended to make sure the power analysis is reproducible (by sharing the code, or a screenshot, of the power analysis), and to provide good arguments for why the study was designed to detect an effect of this size.

For an a-priori power analysis, where the sample size is determined, reporting all information would look like:

An a priori power analysis for an independent samples t-test, conducted using the pwr.t.test function from pwr (Champely, 2020), indicated that for a Cohen’s d = 0.5, an alpha level of 0.05, and a desired power level of 80% required at least 64 participants in each group.

A sensitivity power analysis for an independent samples t-test, conducted using the pwr.t.test function from pwr (Champely, 2020), indicated that with 64 participants in each group, and an alpha level of 0.05, a desired power level of 80% was reached for an effect size of d = 0.5.

How Ethics Approval Check works

This module searches for statements of ethics approval, IRB approval, institutional review board approval, and related terms.

Patterns are designed to capture:

Human research committee names - Institutional Review Board / IRB (US standard) - Independent Ethics Committee / IEC (ICH-GCP international clinical trial term) - Ethical/Ethics Review Board (generic) - Ethics (Advisory/Review) Committee/Board/Panel/Sub-committee - Research Ethics Committee/Board/Panel (UK/Canada/Australia) - Medical Ethics Committee - Human Subjects/Research Committee / Human Research Ethics (Advisory) Panel - Committee for/on the Protection of Human Subjects - Committee on (Health) Research Ethics - Office of Research Ethics - Abbreviations: IEC, REC, REB, ERB, METC, DEC. REC/REB/ERB/DEC are short enough to collide with unrelated jargon (e.g. REC as a recognition-heuristic model abbreviation, DEC as a decision-task label, Reb as an author surname), so these four require an ethics/approval/committee/board/panel/institutional/ protocol word within ~30 characters in the same sentence. IRB and IEC are kept unconstrained as they are not observed to collide in practice. - Dutch: Medisch-ethische toetsingscommissie (METC) - French: Comité d'éthique / Comité d'Ethique

Animal research committee names - Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee / IACUC (US standard) - Any sentence containing animal(s) and committee (or board/panel) within ~5 words of each other — catches Experimental Animal Care and Use Committee, Animal Ethics Committee, Animal Welfare Board, Animal Welfare Ethical Review Body, etc. - UK: Animal Welfare Ethical Review Body / AWERB - Dutch: Dierexperimentencommissie / DEC (see DEC constraint above); Instantie voor Dierenwelzijn / IvD - German: Tierversuchskommission / TvK (advisory committee under §15 Tierschutzgesetz) - French: Comité d'éthique en expérimentation animale / CEEA; Comité d'éthique animale - Spanish: Comité de ética en experimentación animal / CEEA

Approval phrasing - Ethics/Ethical (approval|clearance) [was] (granted|obtained|received|given|secured) - Ethically approved - Approved by the (ethics|irb|institutional|independent|local|review|research) ... - This/The study/protocol/experiment/procedures/methods/research [was] (approved|reviewed) by - Study was ethically approved - (Followed|met|follow) the ethical (guidelines|standards|requirements) - In accordance with ... ethical (guidelines|standards) - Ethics protocol - Approved under (protocol|reference|number)

Waiver / exemption - Ethics/Ethical (waiver|exemption) - IRB (waiver|exemption|exempt) - Exempted from (ethics|irb|institutional review) - Waived by the (ethics|irb|institutional|review) (committee|board) - Deemed exempt

Declaration of Helsinki - Declaration of Helsinki / Helsinki Declaration

Patterns are NOT designed to capture: - General ethical considerations or ethical dilemmas in study content - Mentions of “ethics” in theoretical or philosophical contexts - Author approval of a manuscript (e.g. “all authors approved the final version”) - Ethical behaviour as a measured variable (e.g. “ethical decision-making”) - URLs containing “ethics” in path components

Live data collection is detected by the internal helper .detect_live_data(), which searches for sentences indicating that data were collected directly from human or animal participants. Papers without any live-data signal are not flagged as missing ethics approval. The helper uses conservative patterns to avoid flagging secondary data analyses, computational experiments, or economics papers that analyse existing survey data. See .detect_live_data() for the full list of patterns and intentional exclusions.

A paper that states that no human or animal subjects took part (“no human subjects were involved”, “did not involve human participants”, or people taking part “not as subjects”) is not expected to have an ethics statement, whatever participant phrase it also has (see .detect_no_participants()). A negation near a participant phrase alone (“No participants were excluded”) does not count: it reports a study with participants.

How Power Analysis Check works

This module uses regular expressions to identify sentences that contain a statistical power analysis. If specified by the user, it also uses a large language model (LLM) to extract information reported in power analyses, including the statistical test, sample size, alpha level, desired level of power, and magnitude and type of effect size.

The Power Analysis Check module uses regular expressions to identify sentences that contain a statistical power analysis. Without the use of an LLM, the module uses regular expressions to classify the power analysis as a-priori, sensitivity or post-hoc. With the use of an LLM, it checks if the power analysis is reported with all required information.

The regular expressions can miss power analyses, or fail to classify them correctly. The type of power analysis is often difficult to classify, which can easily be solved by explicitly specifying the type of power analysis as ‘a-priori’, ‘sensitivity’, or ‘post-hoc’. Note that ‘post-hoc’ or ‘observed’ power is rarely useful. The LLM can fail to identify information in the paper, and will not have access to information in paragraphs in the paper other than those that contain the word ‘power’. This package was validated by the Metacheck team on articles in Psychological Science.

How Preregistration Check works

Retrieve information from preregistrations in a standardised way, and make them easier to check.

The Preregistration Check module identifies preregistrations on the OSF and AsPredicted based on links in the manuscript, retrieves the preregistration text, and organizes the information into a template. The module retrieves text from the OSF with its API, and from AsPredicted with ResearchBox’s API when a ResearchBox key is set (see rbox_token()), or else from the preregistration’s web page. How StatCheck works

Check consistency of p-values and test statistics

The Statcheck module runs Statcheck. Statcheck searches for regular expressions that match a predefined pattern, and identifies APA reported statistical tests. More information on the package can be found at https://github.com/cran/statcheck. The module only returns Statcheck results for t-tests and F-tests, as these are the only tests which have been validated, see https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/tcxaj_v1/.

Statcheck was developed by Michèle Nuijten and Sascha Epskamp.

Statcheck considers p = 0.000 an error, as you should report p < 0.001. Furthermore, p < 0.03 is an error if the p-value was 0.031, and one should simply report exact p-values (p = 0.031). Statcheck might miss one-sided tests, and falsely assume the p-value is incorrect. For more information, see StatCheck.

This module only checks statistical results reported in the running text of the manuscript. It cannot (yet) process statistics reported only in tables.

How Exact P-Values works

List any p-values reported with insufficient precision (e.g., p < .05 or p = n.s.) or reported as exactly zero (e.g., p = .000).

This module uses regular expressions to identify p-values. It will flag any values reported as p > ? or p < numbers greater than .001. It will also flag p-values reported as exactly zero (e.g., p = .000, p = 0.00), which are mathematically impossible — p-values are never exactly zero and should instead be reported as p < .001.

A p-value that is not the result of a test is not flagged: a table note (“* p < .05, ** p < .01”), a figure legend (“Asterisks indicate significant differences (p < .05)”, “Values in bold are significant at p < .05”) or a stated threshold (“p < .05 was considered significant”, “thresholded at p < .001”). GROBID often puts such notes into the running text, so each p-value is judged by the words in its own clause (the text up to a “;” or a full stop), and never left out when a test statistic belongs to it (“F(1, 7) = 8.03, # p < .05”). The column not_a_test of the table says why a p-value was left out (“marker”, “threshold” or “legend”). Notes in other forms, such as a marker after the p-value (“p < .05 (*)“), are still flagged.

This module sometimes flags false positives when sentences discuss how p-value thresholds were set. It can also miss an imprecise p-value that is reported against a threshold (“decreased at the p < .05 significance threshold”).

This module only checks p-values reported in the running text of the manuscript. It cannot (yet) process p-values reported only in tables (as opposed to a table’s footnote text, which the module does see and tries to exclude — see above).

This module was developed by Lisa DeBruine and Daniel Lakens

How Codebook Check works

This module checks whether the data columns in a repository are documented in a codebook or README. It locates codebook/readme files, extracts the variable definitions they contain, matches those definitions against the data columns extracted by data_check, and reports documentation coverage: how many data columns are documented, and which documented variables are never used.

The Codebook Check module consumes the columns and file classification produced by data_check (and, transitively, repo_check). Files classified as codebook or readme are parsed with parse_codebook(), which reads structured tables (CSV/TSV/Excel with a variable-name column and a label column), embedded labels in haven files (SPSS/Stata/SAS), and plain text from rich-text formats (docx/pdf/rtf/odt). Embedded haven labels from data files are also harvested directly.

Each data column is then matched against the parsed variable definitions with match_column_labels(), using normalised-name matching with experiment-group scoping, haven-label priority, and rule-based label-equivalence merging.

Beyond the variable label, the module harvests the DDI-style per-variable properties a source supplies: the value labels / code list (the 1=“Strongly disagree”…5=“Strongly agree” mapping — from SPSS/Stata value labels and from a codebook “values”/“coding” column), the missing-value scheme (which codes denote missingness, from haven declared missings and labels that read as “refused”/“n/a”), and question text and columns when present. These are carried onto the matched data columns and exported into the Psych-DS variableMeasured (as a schema.org code list plus namespaced metacheck: fields).

The module defaults to rules-only when llm_use(FALSE). When llm_use(TRUE), three optional LLM tiers run: parsing unstructured codebook text that the rules could not handle, fuzzy-matching still-unlabelled columns to still-unmatched codebook variables, and merging conflicting label definitions into a single canonical label.

How Repository Check works

This module retrieves information from repositories.

The Repository Check module lists files on the OSF, GitHub, ResearchBox, DSpace (PsychArchives and other legacy- and DSpace 7-based installations), Zenodo, Dataverse, Figshare, Dryad, ReShare, 4TU.ResearchData, Mendeley Data, DataONE (Arctic Data Center, KNB, and other member nodes), and LDbase based on links in the manuscript.

Listing a repository’s files never requires an API key, but some hosts answer faster or more completely with one: OSF allows 100 requests/hour unauthenticated versus 10,000/day with a token (see [osf_pat()]), and Dryad enforces a strict per-IP daily quota on repeated listing (see cache below). Dryad is a special case beyond rate limits: listing a Dryad dataset’s files works without a key, but actually downloading file CONTENTS from Dryad fails with HTTP 401 unless a key is set, even for a fully public dataset (see [dryad_pat()] or [dryad_auth()]). Since this module only lists files and never downloads their contents itself, that limitation applies to data_check and code_check (which do download content), not here – see their own documentation.

When a linked OSF page is a registration, its registered_from project (the one it was registered from, which the manuscript itself may never link directly) is also checked: if that project is public its files are listed under its own URL, and if it is closed or otherwise inaccessible the report flags it explicitly, since some registrations only describe their content rather than mirroring it.

How Code Check works

This module retrieves information from repositories checked by repo_check about code files (R, Python, SAS, SPSS, Stata, Mplus, MATLAB).

The Code Check module checks R (also R Markdown and Quarto), Python (also Jupyter notebooks), SAS, SPSS, Stata, Mplus, and MATLAB files, using regular expressions to check the code; JASP files are listed but not read. The language of a Quarto document or a notebook is read from the file itself (its engine or kernel), not from its extension. The regular expression search will detect the number of comments, the lines at which libraries/imports are loaded, attempts to detect absolute paths to files, setwd() and install.packages() calls, and lists files that are loaded, and checks if these files are in the repository; R code is also parsed. The module will return suggestions to improve the code if there are no comments, if libraries/imports are loaded in lines further than 4 lines apart, if files that are loaded are not in the repository, if absolute file paths, setwd() or install.packages() are found, and if R code cannot be parsed.

The regular expressions can miss information in code files, or falsely detect parts of the code as a fixed file path. Libraries/imports might be loaded in one block, even if there are more than 3 intermittent lines. The module was tried out internally on papers published in Psychological Science, but has not been formally validated. There might be valid reasons why some loaded files can’t be shared, but the module can’t evaluate these reasons, and always gives a warning.

The module also checks whether the repository pins the R/package versions the analysis actually depended on: an renv.lock file (parsed for the R version and every locked package + its version/source), a sessionInfo()/sessioninfo::session_info() text dump (matched by filename — sessionInfo.txt/session_info.txt and similar, or embedded in a README), or a groundhog::groundhog.library()/checkpoint::checkpoint() date-pin call in the code (a bare library(groundhog)/library(checkpoint) does not count — the pinning call itself must be present). When none of these is found, the report flags it as a reproducibility gap: package versions may drift between when the analysis was run and any later reproduction attempt.

If you want to extend the package to perform additional checks on code files, or make the checks work on other types of code files, reach out to the Metacheck development team.

Checking code requires downloading its file contents. For a paper whose repository is on Dryad, this never succeeds unless a Dryad API key is set: Dryad requires one to download file bytes, even from a fully public dataset (listing the repository’s files does not need one). Without a key, every Dryad-hosted code file is listed but unchecked, and a warning naming Dryad and ?dryad_pat/?dryad_auth is included among the download failures recorded in the manifest.

How Data Check works

This module classifies the files in a repository into semantic types (data, codebook, code, software, output, supplemental, readme, asset, other) and, for tabular data files, extracts each column’s type and summary statistics.

The Data Check module consumes the file list produced by repo_check (or any files added via local_path). Each file is classified with rule-based heuristics layered over metacheck’s file_category() and file_types: file names matching README / codebook patterns win first, then an extension crosswalk, then format-locked extension overrides. For every file classified as tabular data that is available locally, the module reads the file head, describes each column with data_col_facets() and computes summary statistics for numeric columns. Every sheet of a spreadsheet that holds a table is read: a file with several such sheets has its columns under source_file = "file.xlsx [sheet]", and the sheet column names the sheet of any spreadsheet with several sheets; a sheet of notes or a codebook is treated as documentation when the file has another table. Following DDI, a column is described by orthogonal facets rather than a single type: representation (numeric/text/datetime/code — how it is stored), measurement_level (Stevens: nominal/ordinal/interval/ratio), concept (what it measures: reaction_time/age/gender/likert/id/…), role (identifier/measure/condition/timestamp), unit, and a quality state (ok/empty/constant). Concepts are detected by name+value rules (the demographic concepts via data_check_demographic()); when llm_use(TRUE) the model fills concepts and measurement levels the rules left blank. Qualtrics survey exports are recognised (data_check_is_qualtrics()): their extra header rows are stripped so columns type correctly, and the reserved response-metadata columns (StartDate, Duration, Finished, …) are tagged.

The module defaults to rules-only when llm_use(FALSE): columns the rules cannot resolve (ambiguous 3–20-unique integers, ambiguous character columns) fall back to continuous / text respectively. When llm_use(TRUE), ambiguous columns and unresolved file types (other) are sent to llm() for optional refinement, and every file is assigned a study group (ex1, pilot2, shared, …) so that multi-study repositories can be recognised (used by psychds_check). Without an LLM the study group is left NA (unknown).

Column extraction requires files to be readable from disk. Files fetched by repo_check from OSF/GitHub/etc. without a local copy (file_location is NA) are classified but not column-extracted; pass local_path to point at a downloaded copy of the repository.

For a paper whose repository is on Dryad, this means column extraction never happens unless a Dryad API key is set: Dryad requires one to download file bytes, even from a fully public dataset (dataset listing and metadata do not need one). Without a key, every Dryad file stays classified but un-downloaded, column_n is 0, and a warning naming Dryad and ?dryad_pat/?dryad_auth is included among the download failures. A one-time session message is also shown (see repo_check) the first time a Dryad dataset is encountered with no key set.

Every downloaded text file the authors wrote (code, settings, survey definitions such as Qualtrics .qsf files, documentation, outputs) is searched for keys: API keys, access tokens and private keys, which let anyone who downloads the file use a paid service at the authors’ cost. Of a tabular data file only the column names and first 3 rows of the table as read are searched: its values come from participants, but a key the authors set for a whole survey (as embedded data in a Qualtrics survey flow) is exported in every response. The search uses the rules of the secret scanner betterleaks (https://github.com/betterleaks/betterleaks, MIT licence) of high and medium confidence. A key found turns the traffic light red; the report and the keys table give its file, line and type, and only its beginning. Other files over 25 MB and binary files are not searched. A key is never tested with its provider. Every module’s result has any whole key replaced by “keyremoved” (see module_run()).

How Reproducibility Check works

This module assesses whether a paper’s R and Python code can be run on its data. It works in two phases. The static phase (always run) collects the package dependencies the code declares, rewrites the data-file paths that do not work in the repository as the authors organised it, works out the order the scripts must run in, and diagnoses why any referenced input file is unavailable — reporting what a reproduction attempt would involve and where it is likely to break. The execution phase (opt-in, execute = TRUE) then actually runs the code: it copies the repository into a throwaway directory, optionally installs the declared dependencies into a throwaway library, runs each script in order in an isolated subprocess with a per-script timeout, and captures the outcome and full output of each run.

By default (execute = FALSE) the module does not run any code — it performs only static analysis: reading dependencies with the same extractors as code_check, finding the files the code references in the repository, and building a run-order dependency graph. It reports what would be installed and run rather than doing it.

When execute = TRUE, the module additionally runs the downloaded code on your machine — a deliberate, opt-in action gated behind that argument (callr is required for R code). Each script runs in an isolated subprocess so a crash cannot take down the R session; note this isolates crashes, not the filesystem or network. Installs (when install_missing = TRUE) and the throwaway run library never touch your main R library. Each script’s outcome is one of ran_ok, errored, timed_out, dependency_unavailable, not_parsed, run_by_source (an R script another script runs with source(), which is run only inside that script, since on its own it lacks the packages and objects the sourcing script sets up first), run_by_import (a Python file other files import, a module rather than a script, unless it has an if __name__ == "__main__": block), jags_model (a JAGS or BUGS model saved with an R extension, which another script reads) or not_r_content (a file that is not R at all, such as a JSON or HTML dump); these last two are not run and are not failures. python2 marks Python 2 code that was not run. With sandbox = "docker" such code runs with Python 2.7 (option metacheck.docker_image_py2; FALSE turns this off), which modifications records; it is never converted, since that can change results. defines_functions is a file that only defines functions, which is used, not run (table$used_by names the scripts that use it), and not_analysis is a file of software rather than of the analysis: a package’s code, tests, documentation or setup.py, a copy of a tool, a web application, a script a program wrote (table$not_analysis_why says which); neither is run or counted as a script. docker_failed means the container never started, which says nothing about the script, so it keeps the light from being green but does not make it red. needs_a_person is a script that stopped because it asks a person to choose a file or answer a question, or that starts an app, and needs_internet one that stopped because the network is off in a Docker run, or, with internet = TRUE, could not reach what it asked for; download_too_large is a script stopped for downloading more than the limits allow (see internet), and out_of_memory one that, or a program it started (such as a compiler), ran out of the memory each script may use. Abstract

Since its formulation in 1994, the theory has profoundly influenced clinical practice by providing a physiological framework for understanding safety, social engagement, and differentiated threat responses. In January 2026, Paul Grossman and 38 colleagues published a major critique of the theory in Clinical Neuropsychiatry, arguing that important neurophysiological and evolutionary claims of Polyvagal Theory are untenable. This paper argues that the controversy, as articulated in the current Porges/Grossman debate, and for the last twenty years, has been framed in a way that neither side can resolve because the explanatory variable that would permit adjudication of the central physiological claims of the debate is specified by neither. The paper proposes a revised physiological model of the living autonomic nervous system that distinguishes neural circuitry from the neurochemical conditions under which that circuitry operates. Furthermore, it identifies the primary neuromodulatory chemistry required to distinguish between shutdown, emotional freezing, tonic immobility, and dissociation (the states at the centre of the Grossman/Porges dispute) as the family of endogenous opioids: the one family for which a human clinical literature on immobilization already exists, yet a peptide family omitted from the binary two-pole neurochemical model (social buffering and autonomic flexibility: (oxytocin and urocortins), versus vigilance and allostatic load: (vasopressin and corticotropin-releasing hormone)) PVT has endorsed. With these revisions, observations emphasized by both Porges and Grossman can be understood as accurate relative to the parameters of their descriptions, even though seemingly contradictory, because they are describing different (yet presently unspecified) physiological (neurological and neurochemical) conditions rather than mutually exclusive conclusions. The model generates new physiological predictions that can be tested experimentally and offers a pathway for resolving a controversy that has remained intractable for two decades. Independently, the paper establishes the supradiaphragmatic/subdiaphragmatic partition of the vagus on direct human measurement rather than on comparative inference and asserts the importance of this differentiated conduction architecture being an afferent system.

OVERVIEW

Polyvagal Theory is a systems-level, pathway-specific framework of autonomic regulation developed by Stephen W. Porges, PhD. Since its formulation in 1994, Polyvagal Theory has profoundly influenced clinical practice by providing a physiological framework for understanding safety, social engagement, and differentiated threat responses. The theory takes its name from Porges' differentiation of two neuro-anatomically and evolutionarily distinct vagal systems (poly = many, vagal = vagus) that are both parasympathetic and cardio-inhibitory, yet have distinct neuro-anatomical locations in the body, myelination specifications, and biobehavioral function. This differentiation of two distinct vagal pathways bundled in the common conduit of the vagus nerve updates the neuroanatomical model of autonomic function from a two-term model of autonomic balance (parasympathetic/sympathetic) to a three-term hierarchy (ventral vagal, dorsal vagal, sympathetic) with cascading implications for our understanding of autonomic regulation.

In January 2026, Paul Grossman and 38 colleagues published a major critique of the theory in Clinical Neuropsychiatry, arguing that important neurophysiological and evolutionary claims of Polyvagal Theory are untenable. "Why The Polyvagal Theory is Untenable: An International Expert Evaluation of the Polyvagal Theory" (2026). This is not the first critique Grossman has written, but the culmination of a sustained 20-year critique of the theory, the terms of which are largely unmodified since 2007. Porges responded in the same issue, arguing that the critique does not adequately engage the theory as articulated in the scientific literature. "When a Critique Becomes Untenable: A Scholarly Response to Grossman et al.'s evaluation of Polyvagal Theory" (2026). With Grossman's perspective endorsed by 38 vagal experts, the exchange has sharpened a long-standing controversy without resolving its central physiological disagreement, leaving both researchers and clinicians uncertain whether Polyvagal Theory's central ideas a) remain valid because the Grossman et al. critique has missed the mark (Porges' assertion) or b) have now been discredited (Grossman et al.'s assertion), c) remain substantially defensible with modification (Polyvagal Theory needs substantive revision), d) require conceptual or neurophysiological alterations that would fundamentally alter their character (Polyvagal Theory is superseded because revision is so major it represents a novel theoretical formulation), or e) whether Polyvagal Theory should be regarded as a metaphor. This exchange has produced neither productive field-advancing creative friction nor refined testable theses on either side, but has rather further entrenched a pattern that amplifies a generally unsettled stasis in both the research and clinical domains. This paper argues that the controversy, as articulated in the current Porges/ Grossman debate, and for the last twenty years, has been framed in a way that neither side can resolve because the explanatory variable that would permit adjudication of the central physiological claims of the debate is specified by neither. Polyvagal Theory has made the required observation without raising it to a principle. In 2001 Porges paired an oxytocin-conditioned account of calm immobilization with a vasopressin-conditioned account of immobilized fear at identical dorsal vagal anatomy -the decoupling of chemistry from circuitry, performed once, on one system, and never generalized. It has not been extended to the sympathetic system, whose very name encodes the conflation, and the phenomena that would have forced it -play, recruiting that system's architecture without its characteristic chemistry; intimacy, recruiting dorsal vagal architecture under safety -were absorbed as hybrid states, co-recruitment of two systems rather than one system under two chemistries. That the observation was never generalized follows from the form of the neurochemical model the theory inherits, which retains the two-term structure that its own three-term account of neurology was built to supersede. This is the two-pole constraint: a model with two poles has one axis, so every state it represents must be placed as distance along that line, and shutdown falls at the far end of vigilance rather than outside the line altogether. The chemistry that would place it outside -the endogenous opioids -has no position in such a model, and does not appear in it.

The paper proposes a revised physiological model of the living autonomic nervous system that distinguishes neural circuitry from the neurochemical conditions under which that circuitry operates, together with a specific neurochemical mechanism for the cardiac and respiratory signature of life-threat immobilization, offered as a falsifiable hypothesis and severable from the variable claim it illustrates. Independently of that distinction, the paper establishes the supradiaphragmatic/subdiaphragmatic partition of the vagus on direct human measurement rather than on comparative inference. Electron microscopy of human vagal tissue locates a discontinuity at the diaphragm confined to the myelinated population, with large-calibre fiber present above it and absent below. What the boundary marks is conduction architecture and informational resolution rather than system identity: above the diaphragm temporally precise and spatially specific afferent signalling is available, and below it only the integrated and dispersed. Because the partition is measured rather than inferred, it holds whether or not the formulation built upon it does.

Consequences follow for the evolutionary framing both parties share. Of the three functions phylogenetically ordered dissolution performs in the Polyvagal Theory, the present account substitutes for two -escapability rather than phylogenetic age selects the state, and pathology is patterned by the chemistry coupled with recruited neurology, rather than by reversion along a developmental sequence -and refutes the third, since a state enterable without antecedent mobilization is not reached by sequential traversal. The paper takes no position on whether the phylogenetic account holds. What it establishes is that the clinical claims do not require it, and that the burden of proof for clinical utility therefore need not rest on a reconstruction that cannot be settled.

Within this framework, observations emphasized by both Porges and Grossman can be understood as accurate relative to the parameters of their descriptions, even though seemingly contradictory, because they are describing different (yet presently unspecified) physiological conditions rather than mutually exclusive conclusions. The model generates new physiological predictions that can be tested experimentally and offers a pathway for resolving a controversy that has remained intractable for two decades. The distinctions developed here, and the model that follows from it, arise from Autonomics, a framework of autonomic state set out by the authors in a series of foundation papers. 1

1 -INTRODUCTION

Polyvagal Theory has become one of the most influential physiological frameworks in traumainformed psychotherapy. Recent critiques of its neurophysiological foundations have left many clinicians wondering whether the theory should be abandoned, defended, or revised. The consequences of this dispute extend well beyond the pages of the journals in which it has been conducted. Over the past two decades it has shaped the training of hundreds of thousands of clinicians and, through them, the care of countless patients and clients. The recent exchange between Grossman and Porges has therefore left many practitioners in an uncertain position. If the theory has been fundamentally discredited, should it be abandoned? If it remains substantially valid, what, if anything, requires revision? These are not merely academic questions because they bear directly on how clinicians understand safety, threat, connection, and recovery in the people they serve. For nearly two decades, the debate has stood at an impasse. Grossman argues that PVT's proposed mechanism is inconsistent with current physiology and evolutionary inference, while Porges argues that the critique does not accurately engage the theory as articulated in the research literature. Despite repeated exchanges, neither position has produced a framework capable of experimentally resolving the disagreement over the physiology of shutdown. 2, 3, 4 The authors of this paper have applied Polyvagal Theory clinically for decades across trauma treatment, somatic practice, clinical neurology, and psychotherapy. They regard it as a framework of considerable clinical value, while also recognizing important gaps in its physiological explanation. This paper argues that the long-standing dispute between Polyvagal Theory and its critics persists because neither position has built a category for the variable that would adjudicate it: neurochemical context. The paper traces this distinction in part to the methodological history of neuroanatomy, where neurochemistry and neurology have remained conflated. That the category was never built follows from the form of the neurochemical model the theory inherits, which retains a two-term structure that Polyvagal Theory's own three-term account of neurology was built to supersede. By distinguishing neural circuitry from the pervading neurochemistry in which that circuitry operates, the paper proposes a three-variable model of autonomic state (neuroception/ recruited neurology/ pervading neurochemistry) that reconciles key observations emphasized by both sides, generates new and falsifiable physiological predictions, and reframes a two-decade controversy as a question that can, in principle, be tested directly in living human subjects for the first time. The mechanism proposed is offered as a falsifiable hypothesis; the variable claim does not depend on it.

2 -THE HISTORY OF THE DEBATE AND THE FOUR DISCOURSE LAYERS

Porges published The Polyvagal Theory: Neurophysiological Foundations of Emotions, Attachment, Communication, and Self-Regulation in 2011, consolidating research developed since the theory's formulation in 1994. Clinical uptake was rapid: by the mid-2010s, Deborah Dana and others were translating the theory for clinical and lay audiences. Dana's work popularized the image of an Autonomic Ladder, with ventral vagal at the top and dorsal vagal at the bottom. 5 In 2020, Porges and colleagues founded the Polyvagal Institute to steward the theory's dissemi-nation. By the early 2020s a simplified version -the 'Polyvagal Ladder' -had entered popular psychology and coaching contexts. (See Figure 1).

In order to address the complexity of the clinician's dilemma, it is useful to distinguish four layers of articulation that seek to describe Polyvagal Theory as it now exists in the world. With regard to scientific rigor we identify the research layer of evidence derived from physiological research; the clinical layer of evidence derived from direct practice (represented by the Polyvagal Institute's teaching curricula); the meme layer represented in social media and popular culture; and the applied layer 6 as used in commercially licensed technologies such as those developed with the Canadian neuro-technology firm Unyte (including the listening therapies -Safe and Sound Protocol, Rest and Restore Protocol). These layers are rarely distinguished by the theory's critics, its popularizers, and even its defenders. We assert that confusion about the relative truth value of different layers has stalled productive discussion that could otherwise move both research and understanding about clinical intervention with trauma and illness forward.

Grossman's critique, whatever its merits at the research layer, is frequently read by clinical and lay audiences as a thoroughgoing critique ("debunking") of all aspects of the Polyvagal theory, which has provided valuable guidance in clinical circumstances, as evidenced by clinically significant empirical results. Yet Porges' clarifications about how the critique misses still do not provide adequate guidance for clinicians about the broader implications for practice, or indeed how to counter claims of 'debunking' except in the most esoteric terms. While this discourse fragmentation partly explains why the exchange has been able to run for twenty years without resolution, it does not by itself explain why the exchange has been unresolvable even at the research layer, where both Grossman and Porges are arguing in good faith from their own research and the primary literature. That deeper reason is that while both parties have been well-equipped to argue about anatomy and evolution, neither has systematically applied, in the conceptual vocabulary of autonomic state construction, a category for the variable that would actually adjudicate their central empirical disagreement about the physiology -which is the argument developed in Section 4, after the necessary groundwork of Section 3, and formalized in Section 5.

3 -FOUNDATION ERRORS IN NECROTIC ANATOMY

The methodological concerns considered in this section predate Polyvagal Theory, yet describe the neuro-taxonomy that both Porges and Grossman inherited and are working from. They are relevant to the present dispute for a specific reason. The anatomical maps inherited by contemporary autonomic neuroscience were necessarily constructed under methodological constraints, including the study of postmortem tissue. Those constraints matter here insofar as they encourage fixed functional identities to be assigned to neural structures whose physiological expression in a living organism is context dependent, including depending on variable neurochemical context. The argument that follows invites revision of established neuroanatomy while concurrently asking whether anatomy alone is sufficient to explain autonomic function.

A category error occurs when a claim properly belonging to one logical category is treated as though it belonged to another. 7 The construction of the foundational maps of neuroanatomy has been shaped by a sequence of distinct, self-reinforcing category errors: the conflation of correlation with identity, the conflation of distinct levels of description, the conflation of tissue preservation with in vivo presentation, the conflation of visual differentiability with neurophysiological distinctness, and the granting of causal centrality to structures whose apparent primacy reflects a discipline that has historically located explanatory authority in cognition, rather than any demonstrated primacy in the regulation of autonomic state. Each error compounds the last: a taxonomy built on one mistaken category becomes the substrate on which the next category error is constructed, until the resulting neural cartography reflects the sequence of methods by which the nervous system was studied more than the organization of the nervous system itself.

The central category error this paper diagnoses did not originate with Porges, and it is not a defect of Polyvagal Theory specifically. It is inherited broadly by the discipline of autonomic science from the methodological conditions under which modern neuroanatomy constructed its foundational maps, as detailed at length in Forest Not Lumberyard: Methodological Bias, Structural Error, and a Necessary Transformation of the Epistemic Ground of Autonomic Neuroanatomy. 1 Four compounding conditions are relevant here. Three are summarized here (arguments 1,2, and 4 from that paper) while developed at length there ; the third (argument 3) is elaborated below, because its argument is load-bearing for this paper.

The first is the methodological hegemony of necrotic anatomy: cadaver dissection as the epistemic standard for the originating neuro-anatomical maps in the field. Post mortem study conserves large-diameter myelinated fiber, yet the finest fibers in the nervous system are a hundredth the width of a human hair, and the fine-fiber network through which the living autonomic nervous system receives the majority of its afferent input degrades before it can be observed. 8, 9 The received maps are therefore devoid of the fine fiber that is their instantiation in living tissue: they are maps of autonomic trunkline structure rather than maps of autonomic structure. If the living ANS is an Oak tree in summer, the inherited maps represent winter-bare Oaks. The second is the hegemony of visuality: visual differentiation of gross structure in the cadaver was established as the epistemic standard, reifying structural artifacts as neurophysiological fact, so that nomenclature separates structures which are physiologically and functionally contiguous -the brainstem from the spinal cord -across a transition that distributed autonomic and sensorimotor circuits span continuously.

The fourth is the brain-centric bias, and it is tiered: modern neuroscience is cognition-centric: it grants causal centrality to structures whose apparent primacy reflects a discipline that has historically located explanatory authority in cognition (central-over-peripheral bias). In addition, the autonomic nervous system is roughly 80 percent afferent and the peripheral ANS primarily extra-cranial 10 , yet models of autonomic organization have generally been built around central structures and their motor efferent outputs, partly because differentiating afferent autonomic from somatic fibers has been challenging (output-over-input bias). Langley, who introduced the term 'autonomic' in 1898, defined the system by its efferent pathways, restricting the category to visceral motor fibers and placing afferents outside it. 11 The restriction was definitional rather than provisional: afferents were not omitted pending better methods, they were put beyond the boundary of the category. The boundary has not since been redrawn. What it produces is a nomenclature in which every term names an output, applied to a system four fifths of which (between 73.9% and 80+%) is input -a convention that inverts what the autonomic nervous system is: a refined surveillance intelligence, named for the motor outputs by which it modulates organs, glands and the vasculature. An interoceptive listening and modulation system named only for its modulation componentry: its primary afferent intelligence undifferentiated and except in cases where it is resolved through electron microscopy, largely invisible. Polyvagal Theory inherits and continues aspects of both biases. Every structural term in the framework names an efferent structure or an efferent pathway: the ventral vagal complex and its nucleus ambiguus, the dorsal vagal complex and its dorsal motor nucleus, the myelinated cardiac efferents, the special visceral efferents of the trigeminal, facial, glossopharyngeal, vagus and accessory nerves, the social engagement system, the face-heart connection, the vagal brake. On the afferent side the framework names only termination sites -the nucleus of the solitary tract and, for the ventral vagal complex, the source nuclei of the facial and trigeminal nervesand gives the afferent fibers themselves no structural terms and no partitions. The asymmetry is not an interpretation of the theory; it is a list of its terms. Neuroception, which the theory makes causally prior to all of them (the moment to moment neural detection of safety, danger, or lifethreat), spans every afferent receptor in the body, every fiber that carries their signal inward, and whatever structures integrate them -and is specified at none of those levels. The most consequential revision to autonomic science in fifty years specifies almost none of what the autonomic nervous system is made of.

It also inherits unquestioned the close conceptual association between sympathetic neurology and its characteristic neurochemistry and, in the parasympathetic direction, closely associates the dorsal vagal complex with immobilization. Comparatively less attention has been given to how differing neurochemical environments might alter the functional expression of the same circuitry.

CONDITION THREE: CONFLATION OF NEUROCHEMISTRY WITH NEUROLOGY

The third of these four category errors is the conflation of neurochemistry with neurology, a category already two centuries old when the chemistry arrived to be absorbed into it. In 1904, T. R. Elliott observed that administration of epinephrine (a catecholamine) produced physiological effects resembling sympathetic nervous system activation. 12 The observation was foundational in bridging neurology to neurochemistry. This led to Dale in 1914 characterizing acetylcholine's actions and distinguishing muscarinic from nicotinic effects, noting the resemblance to parasympathetic stimulation, then Loewi's subsequent identification of Vagusstoff (1921), laying groundwork for Loewi and Dale's 1936 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, awarded for discoveries relating to chemical transmission of nerve impulses. Over time, the close association between sympathetic activation and catecholamine release reified a conceptual model where the anatomical system and its characteristic neurochemical milieu are treated as a single functional entity, e.g., the Sympathetic Nervous System.

While the identification of a specific neurotransmitter with a specific pathway proves almost tautological -without noradrenaline at the sympathetic neuroeffector junction there is no sympathetic signal -the categorical claim is something else: that sympathetic names a chemicallydefined identity.

That claim was so entrenched that it went unexamined even when von Euler demonstrated, in 1946, that Elliott had named the wrong catecholamine. 13 The sympathetic transmitter is not epinephrine, as Elliott had proposed: it is norepinephrine.

The correction is more instructive than its reception suggests. Elliott's evidence was an administered substance reproducing a system's functional signature, and the inference drawn from it was identity between that substance and the neurology (e.g., epinephrine is the sympathetic neurotransmitter). Forty-two years later the substance in the inference proved not to be the system's transmitter at all -yet the identity survived, one catecholamine (norepinephrine) substituted for another (epinephrine), its form untouched by the replacement of its content. A claim indifferent to which molecule satisfies it is not a claim about a molecule. It is a claim about where the identity of a system resides. What the field had taken for a finding about physiology was functioning as a convention of classification.

The finding carried a further implication that went undrawn. How could a substance that is not the sympathetic transmitter produce that system's characteristic effects? Only because those effects are determined at the receptor rather than at the nerve. Epinephrine acts at the same adrenoceptors on target tissues through which postganglionic sympathetic fibers signal, and a receptor is indifferent to how its ligand -any molecule that binds it -arrived. Neurotransmission is one means of delivering a ligand to a receptor but it is not what produces the effect. A substance that is not a system's transmitter had therefore been reproducing that system's output for four decades, which establishes that the output was never diagnostic of the pathway. Chemistry and neurology were demonstrably separable in 1946. The demonstration was read as a correction to which molecule holds the title. It was in fact a demonstration that the title had never been the kind of thing a molecule could hold -that what Elliott's experiment could es-tablish, pharmacological sufficiency at a receptor, and what the field took it to have established, the chemical identity of a neural system, were never the same claim.

Epinephrine's own position in the sympathetic system illustrates why a two-term account of that system's chemistry was never sufficient. Endogenously, epinephrine is not a junctional transmitter but a circulating hormone released from the adrenal medulla along the Sympathetic-Adreno-Medullary axis, reaching the same receptors from the bloodstream rather than across a synaptic cleft, and on a timescale of minutes rather than milliseconds. The consequence is clinically familiar: a defensive mobilization resolves on the timescale of its slowest chemical arm rather than its fastest, which is why activation outlasts the event that provoked it by an interval no account of synaptic transmission would predict. The characteristic chemistry of the Sympathetic Nervous System has thus comprised more than one mode of action from the beginning.

A third mode is the one this paper takes as its variable, and the three are distinguished by how they act rather than by where they originate. Neurotransmitters are released at the junction, act within milliseconds, are cleared within milliseconds, and are constitutive of transmission itself. Neurohormones are released into circulation and reach their receptors at a distance. Neuromodulators -among them the endogenous opioids, oxytocin, and corticotropin-releasing hormone -are released from varicosities and dendrites as well as from terminals, diffuse beyond the synapse, act over seconds to hours, and signal through receptors that alter the gain of transmission rather than effect it. They do not carry the signal. They determine what the signal, once carried, produces: not only the magnitude of a response but its character.

These are modes of action rather than classes of molecule, and the same peptide may act in more than one: oxytocin released from the posterior pituitary into circulation acts as a neurohormone, while oxytocin released centrally from dendrites and varicosities acts as a neuromodulator. What the present argument requires is therefore a distinction not between substances but between the ways a substance acts upon a circuit -not between molecules, but between the modes in which they behave. This is what pervading neurochemistry names.

What differs under different modulatory chemistry is not only the magnitude of a response but its kind. The same circuitry, differently suffused, yields states that diverge in affective character and valence rather than in intensity alone. On the account developed here, the modulatory environment is therefore a determinant of what a state is rather than only of how strongly it is expressed -which is why shutdown is not a more extreme mobilization, and is not experienced as one. This third category -how the chemicals act -was unavailable when the taxonomy was fixed. Volume transmission, peptide co-release, and receptor-level modulation of synaptic gain entered the literature well after the nomenclature had settled; the enkephalins were not isolated until 1975. 14 The vocabulary in which the autonomic systems were named was transmitter vocabulary, because transmitter chemistry was the only chemistry the field had available at the time. A taxonomy assembled from one class of molecule will not readily accommodate a second class whose defining property is that it alters what the first class accomplishes.

The identification of a specific neurochemistry with a specific pathway therefore obscures a distinction the field had no means of drawing at the time the identification was made: the same neural architecture may participate in markedly different physiological states depending upon the neuromodulatory environment in which it operates. A similar pattern is reproduced in later interpretations of parasympathetic function, including the dorsal vagal system described in Polyvagal Theory. Anatomical pathways are assigned stable functional identities on the basis of the neurochemical conditions under which they are most commonly observed. Yet that stability does not hold when those conditions change. This is why pervading neurochemistry constitutes a third variable rather than a re-description of the second. Transmitter chemistry is not separable from recruited neurology: it is what recruitment consists of, and a model naming both would be counting one thing twice. Modulatory chemistry is separable -the same circuit, intact and equally recruited, produces different physiological output as its modulatory environment changes -and the degree of that separability is the explanatory space the three-variable model occupies. The distinction is the difference between a model in which anatomy predicts function and a model in which anatomy predicts only what is available to be modulated.

The distinction applies directly to the system this conflation names. The spinal vertebrate architecture conventionally designated sympathetic is one of the neurological systems governing autonomic regulation of viscera, glands and the vascular system, and it houses central pattern generators for hard-wired biobehavioral patterns -including complex patterned movement, visceral regulation, and various autonomic reflexes -in brainstem nuclei and in dorsal horn and sympathetic ganglia chain structures. The pervading neurochemistry that suffuses this architecture when the body detects danger is a separate matter: the circulating catecholamines of the Sympathetic-Adreno-Medullary axis; the glucocorticoids of the Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal axis; and whatever modulatory peptides the context recruits. Naming the architecture and the chemistry with a single term makes the second of them invisible.

The same neurology is regularly recruited outside threat. It is recruited in play. It is recruited whenever centrally pattern-generated movement is required, including hard-wired autonomic reflexes. The neurology comprising the Sympathetic Nervous System has life-sustaining functions that have nothing to do with fight-or-flight, and a nomenclature naming it for one chemical context obscures every other context in which it operates.

Yet this conflation should not come as a surprise. The Roman physician Galen of Pergamon named the concept of sympathy between parts, illustrating it by dissecting pigs and monkeys and extrapolating his findings to human anatomy; the notion was later taken up in the term Sympathetic Nervous System. By 1732 Winslow had popularized the great sympathetic nerve. By the time chemical neurotransmission was established as a systematic principle of the nervous system, with the Nobel Prize to Loewi and Dale in 1936, the reification of the Sympathetic Nervous System as a single entity had stood for two hundred years. The chemistry did not found the category. It arrived to a category already built, and was absorbed into it.

The present model holds instead that many autonomic circuits are context-dependent, their functional expression emerging from the interaction of neural architecture, neuroception, and pervading neurochemistry rather than from anatomy alone. The critical question is therefore not which circuit is active, but how the same circuit behaves under different neuroceptive and neurochemical conditions. Neither party to the present dispute has asked this question. Porges accounts for dorsal vagal shutdown without naming the neuromodulators that would be active in a life-threat response. Grossman tests the prediction that dorsal vagal states produce bradycardia against a clinical literature of emotional freezing and dissociation, accepting Polyvagal Theory's assignment of those states to the dorsal vagal complex -when the assignment is what required examination. What these four inherited errors have in common is that each converts a limitation of method into a feature of the object. The received taxonomy of the autonomic nervous system is a record of what could be seen in dead tissue, named in the vocabulary available at the time of naming, and it has been read ever since as a description of how the living system is organized. Of the four, it is the conflation of neurochemistry with neurology that bears most directly on the dispute this paper addresses. Neural circuitry supplies the architecture through which autonomic regulation occurs. It does not, by itself, determine what that regulation produces.

4 -WHY THE POLYVAGAL CONTROVERSY IS UNRESOLVABLE AS PRESENTLY FORMULATED

Seemingly incompatible assertions by Porges and Grossman et al. may both be true if the appropriate neurochemical context is factored in.

The Autonomics model proposes that engagement of subdiaphragmatic vagal architecture (the Porgesian dorsal vagal system) in the absence of endogenous opioid activity is not by itself sufficient to produce either the profound bradycardia or the respiratory suppression that characterize the state. Instead, we propose that this circuitry participates in ordinary visceral regulation as well as defensive states. Therefore, when Grossman asserts that there is no consistent correlation between activation of this circuit and bradycardia, he is provisionally accurate. 4 The Grossman et al. critique of Polyvagal Theory levels six specific charges: (1) that Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia is not a direct and reliable measure of central vagal outflow to the heart; (2) that PVT's characterizations of the neuroanatomy and function of the Nucleus Ambiguus and Dorsal Motor Nucleus are inaccurate; (3) that PVT's account of vagal evolution is inaccurate; (4) that PVT's characterization of mammalian social behavior relative to non-mammalian vertebrates is inaccurate; (5) that PVT misinterprets earlier seminal physiological literature; and (6) that its core psychological tenets predate the theory's own elaboration. These claims cluster in two primary areas: physiological critique of PVT, and evolutionary critique of PVT, with spillover into PVT's reading of prior literature and assertions about its tenets predating the theory.

The central load-bearing neurophysiological argument the Grossman et al. paper uses to critique PVT is that in clinical contexts, ordinary human emotional freezing and dissociation do not necessarily produce the massive, sudden heart-rate deceleration that Polyvagal Theory's universalizing account of "dorsal vagal shutdown" predicts. The literature Grossman cites on this point is not in dispute. 15 Nor is the RSA critique, that other signals may be more comprehensive registers of vagal tone -a concession made here without reservation, and one that becomes load-bearing in Section 8, where the same methodological argument is turned on the states the dispute is attempting to distinguish. Heart-rate variability was adopted as the field's primary autonomic signal because it offered the earliest robust, non-invasive window into autonomic regulation. 16 RSA's continued prominence has been reinforced by decades of methodological refinement, accumulated normative data, and widespread institutional adoption. Grossman correctly emphasizes that RSA is influenced by multiple interacting physiological variables, complicating its interpretation as a direct index of vagal tone or global autonomic state.

Yet neither of these concessions resolves the controversy, because neither touches the actual point of failure. The point of failure is structural, and is shared by both parties. Both frameworks place primary explanatory weight on neural circuitry. Porges says that life-threat is mediated by a subdiaphragmatic vagal circuit with origins in the Dorsal Motor Nucleus 17 while Grossman argues that activation of this complex doesn't reliably produce bradycardia. 15 Yet these seemingly contradictory accounts may both be true if differentiating neurochemical context is factored in.

THE INSTRUMENT, USED ONCE

It would be inaccurate to characterize Polyvagal Theory as lacking a neurochemical dimension. In April 2026, Porges co-authored a comprehensive review of neuropeptide regulation of the brain-heart axis, and the review demonstrates that the theory has acquired a developed neurochemical vocabulary. 18 What that vocabulary is used for is the assignment to each system of its characteristic molecule: oxytocin to vagal modulation and social buffering, vasopressin to social vigilance and sympathetic activation, corticotropin-releasing hormone to stress reactivity, the urocortins to recovery. This is the identification of a system with its chemistry, performed at the level of the peptide and with a larger cast than the catecholamine formulation it extends.

The review does state a principle of context-dependence, and what that principle governs is worth stating precisely. Identical stressors may be adaptive in one physiological context and maladaptive in another, and the determinant named is the organism's baseline autonomic organization -specifically, the accessibility of ventral vagal cardioinhibitory regulation. 18 That is a claim about which system is available to respond, not about what a given system produces under a given chemistry. Anatomy continues to determine function; context determines only which anatomy the organism begins from.

The genuine decoupling appears once in the Polyvagal literature, in the founding formulation. The 2001 paper pairs an oxytocin-conditioned account of calm immobilization with a vasopressin-conditioned account of immobilized fear, at identical dorsal vagal anatomy. 17 One circuit, two chemistries, two states differing in kind. The observation was never raised to a principle, 19 never extended to the sympathetic system whose name encodes the conflation most directly, and the phenomena that would have forced the extension -play, which recruits sympathetic architecture without fight-or-flight chemistry, and intimacy, which recruits dorsal vagal architecture under safety -were absorbed instead as hybrid states, co-recruitment of two systems rather than one system under two chemistries.

The theory therefore performed the decoupling without generalizing it, and has not turned it on the system where the dispute is located. Why the observation was not generalized is a question the shape of the neurochemical model answers.

THE FORM OF THE NEUROCHEMICAL MODEL

Polyvagal Theory's founding achievement was the replacement of a two-term neurological model -the paired antagonism of sympathetic and parasympathetic, expressed clinically as autonomic balance -with a three-term one. That move is the theory's most durable contribution, and the Autonomics framework retains it.

The neurochemical model has not made the corresponding move. The 2026 review treats four neuropeptides, and they resolve into two poles: oxytocin and the urocortins supporting social buffering and autonomic flexibility, vasopressin and corticotropin-releasing hormone promoting vigilance and allostatic effort. Dysregulation is defined as a shift of that balance toward one pole. The term the review uses for the relation is balance, which appears fifty-six times in its body text. 20 Two further peptides have been added to the 2017 oxytocin-vasopressin formulation without a third position being added to the model. The binary resides in the architecture, not in the number of molecules.

No claim of descent is made here. The oxytocin-vasopressin opposition arose independently, in comparative neuroendocrinology, and owes nothing genealogically to Langley and Cannon. The observation is one of form rather than lineage: the neurochemical model Polyvagal Theory articulates reproduces the same two-term antagonistic structure that the neurological model was constructed to supersede. Paired antagonism is the least expensive structure available for representing a regulatory system, and it recurs for that reason -the same structural attractor that, in the anatomical domain, produced the reified trunk-line maps this paper's companion work addresses. This also accounts for the fate of the 2001 observation. The decoupling performed there was conducted within the two-pole chemistry -oxytocin against vasopressin -and those two poles are exactly what it found: a dorsal vagal state under affiliative chemistry, and a dorsal vagal state under vigilance chemistry. The second is what Polyvagal Theory has named 'dorsal vagal shutdown' ever since, and what the 2001 paper itself calls 'immobilized fear'. Vasopressin was the terminal position the chemistry afforded, and that is where the life-threat state was placed. Reaching the state the clinical record describes would have required a third chemistry, and the model had no position for one. The binary did not obstruct the decoupling. It bounded what the decoupling could find.

WHAT THE TWO-POLE MODEL CANNOT REPRESENT

A model with two poles has one axis. Increasing threat can be represented only as distance along it -the two-pole constraint -and shutdown must therefore be located at the far end of the vigilance pole. That is where the 2001 formulation placed it, at the point where fight-orflight behaviours were no longer an option; and it is where the 2026 review places it still: past the risk zone, under allostatic overload, with the dorsal vagal complex predominating at high challenge intensity. 18 Twenty-five years and a developed neurochemical vocabulary separate the two statements, and the position is unchanged -because the position follows from the shape of the model rather than from its contents. This renders shutdown a quantitative extreme of mobilization rather than a state categorically distinct from it. It is the autonomic ladder's error, reappearing at the level of chemistry: more threat, further along a single line. And it is the specific proposition the clinical record contra-dicts. Shutdown correlates with lowered heart rate rather than elevated, with numbness and interoceptive deficit rather than heightened visceral distress, with immobilization rather than mobilization, with dissociation rather than fear or rage. It is not more of the same. It is different in kind, and a neurochemical model with an affiliation pole and a vigilance pole has nowhere to put it. That inability is not new. It is the one the two-term neurological model had, and the one the second vagus was introduced to resolve. What the chemistry has reproduced is not only the structure Polyvagal Theory superseded, but the particular failure that made superseding it necessary.

THE ABSENT PEPTIDE FAMILY

In the 2026 review -a comprehensive treatment of neuropeptide regulation of the brain-heart axis under stress and threat, running to some three hundred references -the endogenous opioid system appears once, as a mediator of ischemic preconditioning, in a passage unrelated to defense. The terms enkephalin, endorphin, dynorphin, naltrexone and naloxone do not appear at all, and neither does dissociation. 21 Two things make the omission specific. The review's stated scope is four evolutionarily conserved neuropeptides, and the opioid peptides are evolutionarily conserved neuropeptides; the criterion admits them. The topic falls within scope by the review's own account, since it proposes that dorsal vagal pathways may mediate immobilization responses under extreme threat and places shutdown on its central figure.

The opioid peptides are also the one family for which a human clinical literature on immobilization already exists. Antagonists have been tested against dissociative and immobilization phenomena in human patients for three decades. An open-label trial reports naltrexone reducing dissociation, analgesia and tonic immobility together, and a systematic review of the comparative literature reports a pooled effect favouring opioid antagonists, with high heterogeneity and possible publication bias. 22 A review of neuropeptide regulation of the brain-heart axis under threat is the natural place for that literature to be weighed. The 2026 review passes over it, and the reason is structural rather than evidential: the questions a model can pose are bounded by the positions it contains, and a two-pole chemistry contains two.

THE CONJUNCTION

Polyvagal Theory identified the circuitry: a subdiaphragmatic vagal pathway, distinct in origin and myelination from the pathway governing social engagement, recruited under life-threat and capable of profound cardioinhibition. Grossman et al. established that the circuitry is not sufficient, since across the clinical literature on human emotional freezing and dissociation its engagement is not reliably accompanied by that bradycardia. 4 Both findings are sound.

The dispute has been unresolvable because each party describes one term of a product. In the absence of endogenous opioids, dorsal vagal shutdown neurology is not sufficient to induce bradycardia. The state requires the conjunction: subdiaphragmatic vagal engagement under neuroception of life-threat, within a pervading endogenous opioid chemistry. Porges measured the circuit and found the state; Grossman measured the circuit and did not. Neither specified the chemistry, so neither result could discriminate.

That inescapable stress recruits endogenous opioid activity in humans is established by antagonism rather than assay. Veterans with post-traumatic stress disorder viewing combat footage showed a thirty per cent reduction in pain intensity under placebo and none under naloxone, with that change discriminating the groups more strongly than any other variable measured; peripheral peptide and hormone assays showed nothing, and the authors concluded central mediation. 23 The placement of that chemistry is also established. The defense cascade model derived from the animal literature assigns unresponsive immobility -parasympathetic dominance, depersonalization and derealization -to the ventrolateral periaqueductal column, and assigns the kappa and mu opioids to that phase, implicating them in immobility and in downregulation of the sympathetic nervous system. 24 The same model names the cardiorespiratory profile of the state as immobilization, bradycardia and apnoea, and attributes it to the dorsal vagal complex. 24 Both terms occupy the same phase of the same model, and the model does not join them. The present account joins them.

The claim is correspondingly narrow: it specifies that the state is a product of two terms rather than a function of either, and that the second term has gone unspecified while the first was adjudicated. The cardiorespiratory signature of the conjoined state has never been measured against opioid activity in a living human being. This synthesis now provides a predictive matrix whereby the standing twenty-year controversy between Porges and Grossman can be adjudicated in living human subjects for the first time.

FREEZING, SHUTDOWN, AND DISSOCIATION

A central source of confusion in the Porges/Grossman controversy is that the key terminology that Grossman and Porges are respectively using to denote the autonomic states and subjective expereinces under consideration do not align terminologically. The states under consideration map the defense cascade in its transition from active defense, through tonic immobility, through shutdown and collapse, but Porges and Grossman's terminology talk past one another.

Grossman uses the convention of a term called 'emotional freezing', which might seem to align with Porges use of the term freezing, but does not. When Porges is talking about 'freezing', he is referring to tonic immobility. These terms occupy different locations on the defense cascade. Tonic Immobility refers to an autonomic state where the body is rigid and muscle tone remains high, and the heart shows a deceleration dominated by parasympathetic drive rather than the profound bradycardia that characterizes shutdown. 25 This state is accompanied by concurrent sympathetic and parasympathetic upregulation.

The respiratory signature of tonic immobility is a matter of record rather than inference, and it is not what the term suggests. Tonic immobility is bradypnoeic and regularized, not apnoeic. Plethysmography in the mouse finds that where active exploration produces irregular breathing between three and twelve hertz, tonic immobility drops the frequency into a narrow band near four hertz while the variability of both frequency and tidal volume collapses. 27 Thermocouple recording with simultaneous olfactory bulb local field potentials finds the same: during tonic immobility respiration becomes steady with little cycle-to-cycle variability. 28 The two laboratories differ on the causal role of olfactory input and agree on the pattern. The distinguishing feature is regularity rather than rate. Breathing in tonic immobility is metronomic; shutdown breathing is absent. The absolute frequencies are in a mouse model and do not transfer: the pattern does.

The regularization has a mechanism, and on the account given here it is the same chemistry. The variability of the breathing rhythm derives from the many weak inputs that can carry the generating network to its bursting threshold -what Feldman terms the lability of the rhythm. 29 Opioid action slows the emergent process by which that network assembles toward synchrony, and raises dose-dependently the threshold at which an input triggers a burst. Under moderate opioid load the phasic inputs that ordinarily vary breathing from breath to breath no longer reach that threshold while a tonic mobilizing drive still does. What survives is a rhythm driven by a single steady input: narrow in frequency, uniform in volume, and clinically forced. Under heavier load the tonic drive also fails, and the rhythm stops. This is the structure the cardiac account already describes. In tonic immobility the heart is sustained humorally while neural cardioacceleratory drive is suppressed; the breath is sustained by tonic drive while phasic modulation is suppressed. What the blockade removes in both cases is the modulated component. Tonic immobility is not partial shutdown. It is the selective loss of the variable component of two outputs, and a single parameter -the threshold at which an input produces an output -orders all three states: many inputs reaching threshold in the ordinary case, one in tonic immobility, none in shutdown.

Shutdown is characterized by loss of muscle tone, profound physiological slowing, analgesia, and -on the clinical observation reported here -apnea. 30 No vagal account of the state predicts all four. One feature of the respiratory component distinguishes a failure of drive from an obstruction of its expression, and it is clinically legible. Where air hunger is present, inspiratory drive is being generated and its expression is being opposed. Where air hunger is absent, drive is not being generated -which accounts for the clinical observation that the deepest presentations are not experienced as suffocation. Air hunger is read from the physiology rather than reported, its presence appearing as struggle and its absence as indifference. The authors' clinical observation is that this distinction governs which interventions the state will accept. 30 Clinical observation across two decades of practice indicates that shutdown is entered by two routes, and that the distinction between them is not visible in the state itself. In the first, threat converts from escapable to inescapable while active defense is already in progress: escape is inhibited, the state shifts into tonic immobility with muscle tone high and catecholamines circulating, and collapse follows as the circulating pool clears. In the second, shutdown is the default defensive configuration, and a minor or objectively trivial threat produces it directly. There may be a brief catecholamine registration, but active defense is not an available pathway -the system is conditioned to bypass it, 31 typically from an early period in which expressing active defense produced worse outcomes. No mobilization is suppressed, because none was mounted. The epistemic status of this distinction is clinical observation: it is strong evidence for the existence of the two presentations and for the discrimination being one an observer reliably makes, and it is not evidence for their prevalence.

The consequence is a prediction about recovery rather than about state, and it is the sharper of the two. Recovery from shutdown entered through mobilization requires a context capable of discharging that mobilization, and the person returns through the rage or terror that was suppressed. Recovery from shutdown entered directly reveals no such mobilization, because none was suppressed, and the state can reorganize into connection without passing through activation at all. The mobilization is physiological; its discharge is physiological. Describing a mobilization is not the same act as moving it, and a verbal account of one, while evoking its physiological salience, does not constitute its discharge. Where no context adequate to that physiological discharge is available, the arousal released by resolution has nowhere to go, and the state does not return to baseline. What must occur occurs in the body. It does not occur in language, even though language can evoke its salience.

The phenomenology clinicians record in shutdown converges on the phenomenology of opioid administration: a surreal or dreamlike quality, uncertainty as to whether one is awake, a sensation of floating, and -most specifically -analgesia. 30 The last of these is the one that discriminates. Derealization has many causes. Analgesia has few, and none of them is anatomical: no property of a vagal pathway, dorsal or ventral, predicts that a person sustaining injury will not feel it. Stress-induced analgesia is a well-characterized phenomenon with both opioid-dependent and opioid-independent forms. 32, 33 An account that locates shutdown in anatomy alone leaves peritraumatic analgesia unexplained.

Stress-induced analgesia is a well-characterized phenomenon with both opioid-dependent and opioid-independent forms, and what determines which obtains is the nature of the stressor -the opioid form associated with inescapable stress and with the same ventrolateral column that organizes passive coping, the non-opioid form mediated by cannabinoid signalling in the dorsolateral column. 32, 33, 34, 35 Naloxone-reversible analgesia has been demonstrated in patients with post-traumatic stress disorder responding to combat-related stimuli. 36 An account that locates shutdown in anatomy alone leaves peritraumatic analgesia unexplained. An account that locates it in a pervading opioid chemistry predicts it, and predicts which stressors will fail to produce it.

A second feature of the phenomenology discriminates on the same grounds, and is more readily observed than analgesia because it requires no injury. What patients describe during shutdown is not calm but indifference: the circumstance is registered and does not matter. That is the affective signature of µ-opioid action, in which the sensory component of an aversive state per-sists while its motivational force is removed, and it is not the signature of parasympathetic quiescence, which presents as ease rather than as detachment. No efferent vagal pathway predicts motivational indifference, any more than it predicts analgesia. The distinction also accounts for a feature of the clinical presentation that would otherwise be puzzling: a state that presents subjectively as equanimity is not one people seek help for, and its prevalence is correspondingly underestimated.

By 2020, Porges' model had already introduced tonic immobility as a hybrid state, 37 structurally distinct from the four pure single-system states of which dorsal vagal shutdown is one -this taxonomic separation existed years before Grossman's critique was published. What the framework did not supply was the neurochemical specification that would let the distinction perform empirical work. Absent it, tonic immobility and shutdown remain distinguished by recruited efferent neural circuitry but not by pervading neurochemistry, and the theory does not generate differentiated physiological predictions despite distinguishing the states descriptively within its taxonomy.

Dissociation is something different again. It describes a person's subjective experience -a sense of disconnection from the body, the environment, or the self. Dissociation may accompany tonic immobility, shutdown, placating, or other autonomic states, but it is not itself an autonomic state.

WHAT THE ACCOUNT PREDICTS

This account is testable in a way the current debate is not, because it generates distinct predictions for each state rather than treating anatomy as the sole predictor, and because the variable that discriminates between them can be specified in advance of the experiment.

That variable is escapability, not threat magnitude. Confirmed neuroception of life-threat under an escapable condition should produce transient and non-opioid analgesia, and leave the heart accelerated. Confirmed neuroception of life-threat under an inescapable condition should produce long-lasting opioid-dependent analgesia, and the cardiac and respiratory profile described above. 38, 39 The respiratory predictions are the most readily tested and require no pharmacological intervention at all. If shutdown is apnoeic and tonic immobility is regularized, the two states are distinguishable at the bedside with a respiratory band 40, 41 -independent of cardiac measurement, and independent of any judgment as to which neural pathway is active. The authors' clinical observation is that the distinction holds. It has not been formally measured yet.

A consequence follows for the measurement literature, and it follows from Grossman's own methodological argument rather than against it. Grossman's position, which this paper concedes, is that respiratory sinus arrhythmia is confounded by respiratory parameters and cannot be read as a direct index of vagal tone. The states under dispute are precisely those in which those parameters change systematically: regularized and slowed in tonic immobility, absent in shutdown. Respiratory sinus arrhythmia in these states is therefore not merely noisy but systematically biased in a state-dependent way -a limitation that is structural in the index itself, which measures the cardiac response to respiratory modulation without measuring the respiratory signal 40 -and the direction of the bias differs between the two states the dispute is attempting to distinguish. In an apnoeic state the case is stronger still: there is no respiratory frequency, and the index is not imprecise but undefined. Protocols that have sought dorsal vagal bradycardia using respiratory sinus arrhythmia as the index of vagal engagement would, in the state this paper describes, be applying a measure that collapses for reasons unrelated to vagal tone.

Each of these predictions is falsifiable in a living subject using measures already available. Sympathetic tone can be indexed independently of vagal tone, and respiration can be measured directly. None of them requires resolving what happened in the vagus nerve of a fish four hundred million years ago.

A significant portion of this twenty-year debate -the evolution of vagal control from cardiorespiratory synchrony in fish to RSA in mammals, the phylogenetic status of myelinated versus unmyelinated cardioinhibitory fibers, the recapitulationist logic both PVT and its critics have accused each other of -has been conducted on evolutionary terrain. Evolutionary claims about vertebrate lineages separated by hundreds of millions of years cannot be tested in a living organism, by either party, under any protocol. The three-variable model does not settle the debate by being a better theory of vagal evolution. It settles the specific bradycardia dispute by relocating the question to something that can be directly observed in a living human being in real time -which evolutionary inference, by definition, cannot be. A second relocation is available on the same ground, and Section 5 makes it: the partition of the vagus at the respiratory diaphragm, which both parties have argued comparatively, can be measured directly in human tissue. The terrain of the dispute is escapable in two directions -toward what a living subject can be observed to do, and toward what a human nerve can be shown to contain.

Grossman's critique, for all its anatomical detail, does not fully penetrate the problem, because it is conducted on much the same terrain Polyvagal Theory itself is built on: comparative anatomy and evolutionary inference across species. The critique does not fully penetrate the problem because it is a consequence of asking a question -which nucleus, which fiber type, in which species -that does not reach the level at which the current model proposes the error exists.

WHAT POLYVAGAL THEORY CONTRIBUTED, AND WHAT IS PROPOSED IN ITS PLACE

The clinical phenomenon Porges set out to explain in 1994 was not new. A cardioinhibitory, immobilized presentation, distinct from the cardioacceleratory, mobilized presentation of fightor-flight, had been documented clinically since shell-shocked soldiers returned from the First World War. What Polyvagal Theory contributed was not the observation, which predates it by nearly a century, but a plausible neural mechanism for it: a phylogenetically-ordered dissolution, in which a brainstem structure older in evolutionary terms than the one governing social engagement took over under sufficient threat. The mechanism was clinically plausible, and it was this plausibility, anchored to a neurological explanation, that carried the theory's rapid ascent through clinical practice.

What the present model offers in its place is not a different pathway but a different kind of explanation. This distinction changes the scientific question. Grossman's critique asks which brainstem nuclei or vagal pathways mediate bradycardia, which is an appropriate question if anatomy alone is assumed to determine function. The present model asks a different question: how does neurochemical context alter the physiological expression of the same neural circuitry? Because these are different mechanisms, anatomical evidence alone cannot distinguish between them.

The advantage of the present model is threefold: (1) it aligns more closely with clinical observation, (2) it generates predictions that can be tested directly in living human subjects, and (3) it accounts for the immobilized, cardioinhibitory presentation without requiring a phylogenetic explanation. Of the explanatory work the dissolution account performs, the present account substitutes for two parts: the state is selected by whether escape is available rather than by the evolutionary age of the structure recruited, and patterned degradation follows from the chemistry rather than from reversion along a developmental order. The third part, a fixed sequence of recruitment, it does not substitute for but contradicts, since a state enterable without antecedent mobilization is not arrived at by traversal.

This paper takes no position on whether the phylogenetic account holds, and the third advantage is that the question need not be settled: the central hypothesis does not depend upon reconstructing the evolutionary history of the vagus nerve, and can be evaluated instead by examining how neural activity and neurochemical state interact to produce autonomic responses in real time in living humans. The burden of proof for clinical utility is thereby detached from a reconstruction that cannot ever be demonstrated in a living subject.

The untenability verdict does not follow, because neither side has directly tested the condition that the present model identifies as critical: confirmed life-threat accompanied by evidence of endogenous opioid activity.

WHAT THE ACCOUNT DOES NOT CLAIM

What breaks the stasis in the Polyvagal controversy is the claim that pervading neurochemistry is a determining variable in whether bradycardia occurs at all. Were the opioid account proposed here to prove wrong in its particulars, the structure of the disagreement would be unchanged: both parties would still be adjudicating an anatomical question whose answer is conditional on a variable neither has specified, and the protocols that have failed to elicit dorsal vagal bradycardia would still have failed for want of a precondition rather than for want of a pathway. The mechanism is offered as falsifiable. The variable is what the paper asks the field to adopt. This paper does not attempt to resolve the comparative anatomy of the nucleus ambiguus and dorsal motor nucleus across species, nor adjudicate the evolutionary claims regarding cardiorespiratory coupling from fish to mammals. It declines those questions because its claims do not require their answers, not because the answers are unavailable: the account advanced here holds whichever way the comparative record is read. Nor does it contest Grossman's critique of RSA as a vagal tone index, which the authors concede and, in Section 8, extend. Because the account specifies the chemistry rather than the efferent population, it is indifferent to which vagal nucleus carries the resulting cardioinhibition -and Grossman's correction on that point, that the vagal response to defensive immobilization is mediated primarily by the nucleus ambiguus rather than the dorsal motor nucleus, is accommodated rather than contested here. Where PVT and its critics disagree on matters outside the neurochemistry/neurology distinction, this paper does not take a position.

5 -THE VENTRAL VAGAL COMPLEX IS AN EFFERENT ANATOMY

The preceding section located a category error at the output end of the model: a neurochemistry conflated with the neurology it accompanies. A second error of the same structure sits at the input end. The first can be repaired by enriching the chemistry, while the second reaches the term on which the whole framework rests.

The human vagus is predominantly a sensory nerve. Immunofluorescent classification of axons in the mid-cervical vagus of eight human body donors finds sensory fibers to constitute 73.9 ± 7.5 per cent of the right nerve and 72.4 ± 5.6 per cent of the left, against 13.2 ± 1.8 and 13.3 ± 3.0 per cent for special visceromotor and parasympathetic fibers combined. 10 Roughly three fibers in four carry information inward. Polyvagal Theory's two vagal categories are named for two motor nuclei. 42 The ventral vagal complex takes its identity from the nucleus ambiguus and its myelinated cardiac efferents, extended through the special visceral efferents of the trigeminal, facial, glossopharyngeal, vagus and accessory nerves to form the social engagement system; the dorsal vagal complex takes its identity from the dorsal motor nucleus and its unmyelinated visceromotor projection. Every structural element in that architecture is efferent. A taxonomy derived from the organization of motor output is thereby applied to a nerve that is three-quarters afferent.

What the boundary marks, however, is calibre, and calibre is not the afferent-efferent division. Given that roughly a third of cervical vagal fibers are myelinated while parasympathetic and special visceromotor fibers together account for thirteen per cent, a substantial myelinated afferent population follows arithmetically. 10 The myelination gradient cuts through the sensory system rather than around it. This is consequential for what the boundary means in experience. Myelination determines conduction velocity and temporal fidelity; unmyelinated fibers conduct slowly, disperse in time, and converge heavily, with some twenty-two axons to a single Schwann cell within a Remak bundle. 43 A myelinated afferent delivers a temporally precise and spatially specific signal. An unmyelinated afferent field delivers an integrated one. The precision with which a facial expression can be specified and the diffuseness of a gut feeling are not two registers of description. They are two conduction architectures, and the discontinuity at the diaphragm is the point at which the first is no longer available. Supradiaphragmatic and subdiaphragmatic territories therefore differ sharply in their capacity for precise sensation and precise motor control, and they are served throughout by a continuous fine afferent field. One sensory system, variably resolved, reporting to one integrating structure and driving two output systems of markedly different speed and specificity.

NEUROCEPTION HAS NEVER BEEN GIVEN AN ANATOMY

Every structural term in Polyvagal Theory is efferent: the ventral vagal complex, the dorsal vagal complex, the social engagement system, the face-heart connection. Neuroception, which names the process on which all of them depend and from which, in the theory's own ordering, they follow, is specified functionally -a detection of safety, danger and life threat proceeding below the threshold of awareness -and has not been given a structure. 44 The absence is not an oversight, and further work will not close it. It follows from the organization of the afferent system itself.

The efferent vagus divides into two nuclei with distinct projections, and that division is what permits a two-complex taxonomy. The afferent vagus does not divide along any comparable line. In vivo two-photon calcium imaging of the nucleus of the solitary tract in the mouse finds its neurons topographically organized on the basis of body position, with organ representations reflecting their physical positions within the body; stimuli arising in different organs are segregated, while different inputs from the same organ converge. 45 Single-cell profiling of vagal sensory neurons across seven organs resolves their coding into three independent dimensions -visceral organ along the rostral-caudal axis, tissue layer along the surface-lumen axis of each organ, and stimulus modality -together constituting a genetic representation of the body's internal space. 46 Both studies are in mice; the viscerotopic principle is established across species, and no comparable human mapping exists. The vagal afferent system is a map of the body's interior, organized by location and by the kind of stimulus a receptor transduces. Its axes are anatomical and modal. None of them is an axis of threat. This is the structural obstacle. Safety, danger and life threat are not positions in an organ-bytissue-layer-by-modality coordinate system; they are a different kind of thing altogether. Safety is not a property present in the afferent signal and awaiting detection. An anatomy of neuroception, had one been written, would have described a single viscerotopic map receiving the whole internal field -one structure, organized by body position, carrying no partition corresponding to the three conditions it is required to distinguish. What follows is not that the organism fails to assess threat. It is that the assessment is not delivered by the afferent system and is not located within it. The afferent field supplies a con-tinuously updated representation of the body's internal condition; what that representation amounts to is composed, under conditions Section 6 specifies. Assessment is distributed.

Of the four conditions under which Porges holds that Polyvagal Theory would be empirically challenged, three are stated as measures of output: that ventral vagal activity should increase rather than withdraw during defensive immobilization; that robust social engagement should occur without ventral vagal regulation; that myelination of the nucleus ambiguus should fail to coincide with the emergence of respiratory sinus arrhythmia. 3 This is a consequence of the gap identified in this section. A theory can be tested only where it has structure, and the structure is all at the efferent end. The conditions are written in the vocabulary of the motor system because there is nothing at the afferent end to write them about.

6 -THE THREE-VARIABLE MODEL

At its core, the Autonomics model proposes that autonomic state cannot be explained by neural circuitry alone. Whereas Polyvagal Theory describes autonomic state as the interaction between neuroception (the perception of safety, danger, or life threat) and the neural pathways recruited in response, the present model adds a third variable: the pervading neurochemistry in which that circuitry operates.

Accordingly, autonomic state is determined by three interacting factors: 47 (1) autonomic temperature (Porges' neuroception), (2) the autonomic system that is primarily recruited -the Connection System (PVT calls this ventral vagal, Autonomics alters its neuro-anatomical boundaries), the Movement System (sympathetic), or the Grounding System (PVT calls this dorsal vagal, Autonomics alters its neuro-anatomical boundaries) -and (3) the pervading neurochemistry acting on that circuitry. These moment-to-moment interactions occur against the background of each individual's accumulated allostatic load (conditioning history), which shapes how the nervous system responds over time.

Where the system is conditioned to enter shutdown directly the mobilized state is skipped entirely. A sequence that follows from the chemistry admits exceptions where the chemistry differs. An ordering intrinsic to the circuitry does not. Section 4 establishes that bradycardia under life-threat requires both terms; what remains to be said here is why the circuitry alone cannot supply it. The Grounding System is active throughout ordinary life: it is not merely recruited in response to life-threat. It participates in digestion, visceral regulation, and other restorative functions. 17, 48, 49 Its presence alone even during neurocepted lifethreat cannot explain the profound slowing of the heart observed during shutdown. In health-creating states, Connection, Movement, and Grounding all provide input to the sinoatrial node together. Under neuroception of danger, Connection's cardioinhibitory brake lifts (the vagal brake), and sympathetic tone accelerates the heart unopposed by Connection -this is fight-or-flight physiology. In contradistinction to the autonomic state hierarchy demarcated by PVT, Autonomics asserts that the only autonomic condition in which a single autonomic neurological system stands alone is life-threat, and it stands alone by elimination of the other two, not by exclusive recruitment. Thus, autonomic state is determined by the changing balance among interacting systems, not by the activation of a single pathway. 50 That pervading neurochemistry is a determining variable in autonomic state is advanced here on three grounds independent of any one mechanism: the demonstration, available since 1946, that a system's chemistry and its neurology are separable; the two-pole constraint, which shows why the field's present neurochemical model cannot represent the alternative; and the single decoupling Polyvagal Theory itself performed in 2001. It is consistent with over a decade of clinical observation across thousands of clients at Hearth Science, where the Autonomics model was developed.

The category error diagnosed in Section 3 is not confined to the bradycardia dispute. It is the same error that has compromised the Autonomic Ladder as Porges' own model evolved between 2011 and 2023. The third variable is not an addition to the model. It is the dimension in which the states under dispute are separated.

The Ladder's earliest form yielded four pure states -social engagement, fight, flight, and dorsal vagal shutdown -each reliant on a single autonomic system under a single neuroceptive condition. 8 -FALSIFIABILITY

Porges' 2026 rebuttal to Grossman articulates four conditions under which PVT would be empirically challenged: if (1) ventral vagal activity were shown to increase during life-threat or defensive immobilization rather than withdraw; if (2) robust social engagement occurred without ventral vagal regulation; if (3) developmental maturation (myelination) of the nucleus ambiguus failed to coincide with the emergence of respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) and age-appropriate social engagement, contrary to the developmental sequence predicted by the theory; or if (4) the observed coordination between respiration and cardiac vagal regulation turned out not to play a meaningful role in autonomic regulation. 3 One clinically observed phenomenon already meets these self-stated criteria. The placate state recruits ventral vagal neurology during life-threat and defensive immobilization, meeting the first condition directly. A second phenomenon falls outside the four conditions altogether, and is the more consequential for that reason. Shutdown entered without antecedent mobilization resolves directly into connection, without passing through activation at all. This is not a passage the Autonomic Ladder permits: a ladder entails sequential traversal, and that is what makes it a ladder. The two-route account given in Section 4 explains why the transition occurs. Where no mobilization was mounted on the way in, none is revealed on the way out, and the state reorganizes under sufficient neuroception of safety and affiliative chemistry rather than discharging an activation it never carried. 51 The observation is clinical, and its counter-instance is specifiable: a presentation entered directly in which the transition to connection cannot be made without passing through activation. That the same circuitry returns to connection by two different routes depending on the chemistry under which it was engaged is not among the conditions Polyvagal Theory names as falsifying, because all four are stated in terms of which system is active rather than the chemistry under which it operates.

Yet the developers of a theory are not necessarily the sole authority on the complete grounds for its falsification. The examples discussed above illustrate a broader point developed in Section 4. If autonomic function depends on both neural circuitry and neurochemical context, then a model that considers only circuitry may generate incorrect predictions whenever the same neural pathways operate under different neurochemical conditions.

The present model therefore makes a narrower claim than either Polyvagal Theory or its critics. It does not seek to resolve questions concerning comparative neuroanatomy or vertebrate evolution. It declines those questions because its claims do not require their answers, not because the answers are unavailable. On respiratory sinus arrhythmia it makes one claim and no more: that in the states this paper describes the index is not merely confounded but, where breathing has stopped, undefined. It proposes that neurochemical context is an independent determinant of autonomic state and that this additional variable generates physiological predictions not produced by existing models. Those predictions can, in principle, be tested directly in living human subjects using contemporary physiological measurements.

9 -CONCLUSION

The dispute between Grossman and Porges has persisted for two decades not because either has reasoned carelessly, but because both have worked within a conceptual framework that treats neural circuitry as the primary determinant of autonomic state. Within that framework, the disagreement cannot be resolved. Grossman's physiological observations and Porges' clinical observations can both be correct while leading to incompatible conclusions because the shared model lacks a mechanism for distinguishing the physiological consequences of the same neural circuitry operating under different neurochemical conditions, specified by neuroceptive conditions.

The present paper proposes that neurochemical context is this missing explanatory variable: the third dimension of autonomic state (First dimension, neuroception. Second dimension, recruited neurology). In this account, what Grossman refers to as 'stress-related immobilization of emotional freezing or dissociation' is both under-specified relative to Porges' terminology, which reflects differentiation between tonic immobility and shutdown (see Figure 3), such that there is fundamental confusion about which terms refer to which states, and is furthermore unable to be effectively differentiated across the dimensions that determine it beyond neuroception and the recruited neural pathways: namely the neurochemical environment in which those pathways operate. Porges has, himself, under-specified in the Polyvagal model the neurochemical conditions for shutdown. Grossman, in asserting that the dorsal vagus does not reliably produce bradycardia, is correct: it will not produce it absent the neuroception of life-threat and the presence of endogenous opioids. Shutdown, like all autonomic states, thus has a neuroceptive, a neurological, and a neurochemical signature. The assertion of untenability therefore fails for the specific reason that Grossman would have to demonstrate that shutdown did not obtain under those three simultaneous conditions, which he has not done. For that matter, neither has Porges, because PVT is missing this dimension of state construction.

It is not simply that PVT fails to cleanly differentiate play, appease, placate, intimacy, and tonic immobility by the existing rule-based structure of its own taxonomy. Rather, these states are the visible edge of the missing dimension distinguished by variable pervading neurochemistry. The inclusion of this neurochemical dimension generates a greatly expanded state taxonomy that more accurately maps to clinical observation, obviates the autonomic ladder, and generates specific physiological predictions that neither Polyvagal Theory nor its current critiques produce. With respect to the Porges/Grossman controversy specifically, it predicts opposite cardiac outcomes under life-threat depending upon whether a pervading opioid chemistry is present or absent, and a respiratory signature that distinguishes the two states without recourse to the heart.

The contribution of this paper is therefore not a new account of comparative neuroanatomy or vertebrate evolution. Where it does make an anatomical claim, that claim concerns human tissue measured directly rather than lineages inferred across species. Nor is the contribution a general defense or rejection of respiratory sinus arrhythmia as a physiological marker, beyond the specific finding that it cannot be computed in a state without a respiratory frequency. It is a more specific claim: that autonomic state cannot be fully understood from neural circuitry alone. Neuroception, neural circuitry and neurochemical context jointly determine physiological expression, and separating those variables transforms a long-standing conceptual dispute into experimentally testable hypotheses capable of being tested and resolved in living persons.

Two findings established here survive the outcome of the dispute entirely. The partition of the vagus at the respiratory diaphragm has been argued for twenty years on comparative grounds -the phylogenetic status of myelinated against unmyelinated cardioinhibitory fiber, the derivation of vagal control from cardiorespiratory synchrony in fish -and it requires none of them. Electron microscopy of human vagal tissue locates the discontinuity directly, confined to the myelinated population, with large-calibre fiber present above the diaphragm and absent below. This is the single point in the entire controversy at which a comparative argument is replaced by a direct human measurement, and no position in the controversy touches it: Grossman contests the phylogeny and the measurement, Porges defends the phylogeny, and the boundary is established by neither route, because neither party has had an instrument that registers it. What the boundary marks is not two systems but two conduction architectures, and the clinical difference between the precision with which a face can be read and the diffuseness of a visceral sense is a difference in available resolution rather than in register of description. Porges' central anatomical intuition is therefore vindicated on grounds he did not have. What is vindicated is the partition and not the taxonomy erected upon it, and the partition does not fall if the taxonomy does. This partition is, furthermore, of the afferent architecture, which, though composing the numerical majority of autonomic fibers (73.9-80+%), has been under-specified and insufficiently visualized, resolved, mapped and differentiated throughout the history of autonomic science: surely ironic given that the interoceptive capabilities arising from its sensory conduction architecture are the primary living intelligence of these systems.

Grossman et al.'s expertise notwithstanding, Polyvagal Theory cannot be definitively demonstrated untenable unless testable hypotheses can demonstrate that its predictions fail to produce the neurophysiological outcomes it predicts in living subjects under life-threat, with the recruited neurology of shutdown, and its pervading neurochemistry. Nor can it be vindicated, Porges' expertise notwithstanding, except by the same: a demonstration that its premises obtain in living subjects. The present paper provides a concrete, experimentally testable series of predictions that can be used to adjudicate what has until now been an unresolvable, stasisinducing, and ultimately academic debate unsettling the field and further separating academic neuroscience from its application in clinical praxis.

If these predictions are supported, the implications extend beyond the specific controversy surrounding dorsal vagal shutdown. They suggest that autonomic state is more accurately understood as an emergent property of interacting neural and neurochemical processes than as the fixed function of particular anatomical pathways. Whether this account proves correct is ultimately an empirical question. The scope of the present paper is not to settle that question, but to formulate it in a way that can now be tested.
